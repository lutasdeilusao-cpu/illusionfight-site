# CHAPTER 15 — Angel and Victory

Jack and Nina had never fought together in the LDI.

They exchanged a look and jumped back at the same time, each to one side, opening distance and angle. Kim jumped too, half a second late, and kept the same distance.

— Kim. — Jack raised his hand. — Here's the deal. We're going all out. You do whatever you can to defend yourself. This game is about feeling. I can't explain how you'll find your power. But I know you will.

— Bring it. I'm not losing to you two.

Jack took the cane in his left hand and held it horizontal in front of his body. His right hand, the metal one, slid from end to end along its length.

— Victory. — Jack. — Shaman.

The cane broke into three parts connected by links, a metal San Tien Kwan, with the curved cane handle on one end and a spear tip on the other. A golden-brown energy ran through the links.

On the other side, Nina laid the sheathed katana across her left palm. She closed her right hand on the grip.

— Angel. Shaman.

She pulled.

The blade came out in segments. Small blades attached to each other by a wire, stretching to almost two meters, and she whipped the air with it. The crack echoed through the empty room. The blade retracted into the sheath on its own, whole again. She held the sword beside her face.

Kim stood for a second looking at both of them.

None of that was what he expected.

Jack moved first.

Slow at first. Then faster. His right hand holding the spear tip, his left spinning the cane handle in a three-sixty, the metal whistling louder and louder in the air. Kim readied for mid-range, where that weapon would do damage.

At the exact striking distance, Jack jumped back.

Kim followed the jump with his eyes.

And didn't hear Nina.

She came from behind like a bullet, the katana stretched straight for the heart. Kim turned his shoulder at the last possible instant. The blade tore his shirt and opened the flesh from his chest to his shoulder.

The blood came hot. The pain came whole.

A second slower and it would have been the heart.

Kim dropped to one knee with his hand on his chest, pressing. *The whistle. Jack spun that to cover her footsteps. The jump was to make me look at him.* The two had set that up without saying it out loud. The first play had been perfect.

Jack was still in the air. He reassembled the weapon back into a cane and hurled it down like a dart. Kim threw himself back. The tip drove into the floor where he had been and opened a wide hole, dust rising.

*Whew.*

He started to get up. He heard a step behind him. He dodged right by instinct and Jack's kick grazed his ear. The two separated, took guard, traded a few probing punches.

— Having fun? — Jack grinning. — Didn't I tell you it was badass? We wasted years not doing this.

— Very. — Kim with blood running down his belly. — By the way, why is the pain in this game so real?

He lunged with a knee to the diaphragm. Jack had known that knee for years. He blocked with his elbow and, with an open hand, hit the cut on Kim's chest.

Kim saw white.

— Nobody knows how they do it. — Jack while Kim staggered back. — But the sensation here is almost the same as out there. A broken leg is a broken leg. It won't hold you, it hurts, it takes you out of the fight. Keep that in mind.

Jack's face turned serious. His eyebrows rose a little.

He didn't need to speak. Kim had already heard it. Nina's footsteps, almost silent, behind him again.

He jumped to the side.

She already knew. She aimed the sword where he was going to land.

— Expand!

The blade opened from the base in segments, like a metal snake, and came slithering after him.

*Shit. Shit. Shit.*

Kim threw himself to the side again. Barely made it.

Nina smiled in a way that was not at all friendly.

— Harden!

The segments locked. The whip became a rigid, giant blade, longer than the room seemed to allow.

— Angel Luminus Blade!

The blade lit up.

A white light so bright it hurt to look at. Nina spun her body in the air with the whole blade coming down in an arc that caught Kim and half the room. Jack saw he was inside the radius and ran the other way.

Kim had nowhere to go.

He raised both hands in front of his body. It was all he had left.

And something appeared in them.

---

The shock was so great the air trembled.

The blade of light hit something Kim was holding, and the two forces stayed there, one pushing against the other. Nina twisted her body with all her weight to drag the blade forward, and Kim was dragged backward, his feet cutting two furrows in the floor, but he didn't fall.

The light was so much he couldn't see what he was holding.

And in the middle of the light, it began to darken.

He knew that place. He had been there before.

It was there that the Guardian had spoken to him in that disjointed way. *No, no, no... no, yes, yes, yes, yes.*

The dark without a floor. The sound of water dripping in a rhythm that wasn't random, but wasn't music either. Kim kept his arms raised, in the same defensive position, and slowly turned his head.

Beside him, sitting on nothing, an old man with a long white beard, feathered hat, and round dark glasses. The dollar-sign chain on his chest. The golden staff floating beside him without anyone holding it.

— Hello! — Shaman Yawanari opened his arms. — What's up, kid? You good? Smooth sailing? What's the word?

— My God. — Kim. — I swore you were a delusion. That I invented you because I was getting my ass kicked.

— Oh. You thought I wasn't real? — The Shaman put his hand on his chest. — I'm offended. I feel oppressed, man.

Kim's arms were trembling. Even from there, he felt that at any moment the blade would pass and cut him in half.

— Talk fast. What do you want? Why are you here? And what do you know about my father?

The Shaman turned his face to the side and started talking to someone who wasn't there.

— Nah, man, he's worried... He's involved, you know? It's the nephew... But I don't think it's the time. You crazy? It's not the time to talk about that with him. — Pause. — No. No. I know. I KNOW.

He turned back to Kim.

— So, dude. Now is not the time to talk about your pops, all right? The time and place will come. I came here to warn you. Shit's getting crazy. They're after you. And when they get here, they'll want to take that thing you call a body. So handle the Primordial Blood business, or you're fucked. — He slapped his own leg, like someone wrapping up a meeting. — That's the only reason I called you here.

Kim was getting weaker. The light began to return at the edges.

The Shaman faded with it, and before disappearing completely, he pointed with his chin at Kim's hands.

— Man. That weapon you chose is top-tier. Enjoy it. If you learn to use it right, it'll serve you well.

---

The light returned.

The weight of the blade came back with it, and Kim saw what he was holding.

A small hatchet.

Small, with the handle wrapped in something that looked too high-tech for a weapon so ancient in shape. The blade held Nina's Luminus Blade, but the edge of his blade was thin, and the Luminus Blade was wide and heavy and winning.

Kim decided to risk it.

He bent his body backward into a bridge, his back almost touching the floor, and let the blade pass over him.

It passed.

And kept going, along the now-clear path, straight toward Jack, who hadn't run far enough. Jack thrust his cane in front in defense. It deflected the brunt of the strike. The tip of the light caught the top hat.

A piece of the top hat fell to the floor.

Kim let out his breath. Before he could say anything, Jack yelled.

— FUCK, NINA. You cut my top hat. Do you know how much LT I'm going to spend to fix this? Or did you forget we're on the same team?

— My bad. I'll give you the LT. — Nina without taking her eyes off Kim. — Jack. He manifested a personalized weapon. In the middle of the fight.

Jack stopped looking at the top hat.

— He what?

— These weapons are one-of-a-kind. I never managed to pull one. I spent an absurd amount on packs until I found Angel.

— Without spending points? First try? — Jack scratching the beard that wasn't his. — He's high on the SDR... Maybe in a training room the system unlocks it on its own. But it's not an official fight, so... I don't know. I didn't see how it happened.

Kim realized the two were talking about him. He took a step toward them.

The two looked at him at the same time. And jumped back, opening distance again.

*The fight wasn't over.* Kim thought that was very unfair. They could chat and he couldn't. Then he remembered: in a street fight, the only rule was that there were no rules.

He was dying to tell what had just happened. But there was no time, and he wasn't going to disrupt the fight.

He used the time to really look at the weapon.

The shape was of an ancient hatchet, like those stone-bladed ones he had seen in a schoolbook. But the blade was not stone. It was a strange metal, and the edge was not sharp. It wasn't made to cut. It was made to strike. The handle molded to his four fingers as if it had been cast from his hand. Just below his hand, a small pulley with a steel cable wound around it, attached to the end of the handle.

On his belt, which he didn't remember having, hanging on the left side, another identical hatchet.

And on the handle of the one in his hand, engraved in the metal, a word.

*Xakixi.*

Kim remembered Jack sliding his hand along the cane. Nina pulling the sword from the sheath. The name and the word the two had said.

He pulled the second hatchet from his belt. The pulley locked under his hand on its own.

He extended his left arm to the side. Raised his right.

He struck one blade against the other.

— Xakixi. — Kim. — Shaman.
