# CHAPTER 12 — What a Twelve-Year-Old Girl Does

The backyard was the way it always was. Destroyed.

The house was simple, and it got worse every year, because there was never money left for even the most basic repair. But it was a roof. And it had a warm bed.

Kim opened the door.

The mess was the same as the morning. Helena hadn't lifted a finger. Not even to throw the stuff on the floor in the trash.

He took off his backpack, left it behind the door, and started.

Slowly, but he started. What he did every night he was home. Pick up what was on the floor. Wash all the dishes. Sweep. Straighten the furniture, gather the clothes, put them to wash. Every night. Except when he or Jack picked up some side work.

---

Helena came down the stairs.

Dressed up. Another night out with her friends. Purple hair tied back, makeup done.

She looked at Kim with a slightly embarrassed face, went to the kitchen, grabbed a beer, and came back to the living room. It was always like this. Before leaving, she'd pass by him and say goodbye. The only moment of the day the two crossed paths. And it was always the time to ask for money.

Kim was holding the trash bag, picking up the last things from the floor.

— Hey, you piece of shit. Hope you brought cash. Do a DIX for me, I'm already heading out to party.

From a young age, Kim knew: if he had nothing to give, things could get ugly. When he was a kid, it could even mean a beating.

Every day he made a sulky face, got the money, and handed it over.

Today he smiled.

— I do have some, actually. But I'm not giving it to you before you answer a question. — Pause. — And I want to make it clear: I never asked you this. Never even cared. But today some weird shit happened. And now I need to know.

Helena lit a cigarette. Impatient.

— What do you want to know, you wretch? Where I'm going? With who? What I'm going to do? You know damn well that's none of your business. I don't stick my nose in your affairs and I won't accept you sticking yours in mine.

Kim's smile grew. Then vanished.

— It's not that, Ms. Helena. I want to know about my father.

---

She froze.

Almost seventeen years. In almost seventeen years Kim had never asked about his father. She'd always found that strange. But since she preferred not to touch the subject, it was comfortable.

Kim took the cigarette from her hand. Brought it to his mouth. Took a deep drag. Looked her in the eye.

— I know I never cared about the guy. But today something strange happened. Someone, for some reason, seemed to know who he was. I don't know if it was a dream, or some desire I never faced. But now I want you to tell me something about the son of a bitch who put me in this world and disappeared. Anything.

Helena sat on the couch.

Her purse fell to the floor. She lowered her head for a few seconds. Took a deep breath. When she looked up, her face was blank.

— I didn't understand what you're talking about, boy. And I don't care. If it were up to me, you'd never know a thing about your shit father. Your only job here is to work and bring me money every day. You owe me a debt, because I raised you. Now give me the money so I can go.

Kim was surprised.

She'd spoken with such certainty, such sincerity... What kind of thing had this guy done to make her feel so much hate?

But Kim wasn't one to give up.

He sat in a chair in front of the couch. Clasped his hands in front of his mouth, fingers forming a triangle. Lowered his head to breathe. Raised it.

— Ms. Helena. I can't imagine what kind of bastard this guy was. But I have the right to know. I want to know. I need you to tell me. You're not leaving this house today. And I'll never give you another cent if you don't tell me who he is.

---

Anger rose in her face.

— Boy, you better not test me. When I say something, you do it. You're not going to be like the other son-of-a-bitch men I've met in this life. I speak, you listen, and you obey. And don't make me repeat myself. — Her voice rising. — IF IT'S UP TO ME, YOU'LL NEVER KNOW WHO YOUR FATHER IS!

She stood up, huffing.

Kim stayed seated. Hands still in a triangle.

He knew his mother. At that moment reason no longer lived in her. She turned into an animal. She didn't talk. She defended herself with everything she had.

— You piece of garbage! Son of Satan! You have no idea what I went through to keep you alive, to feed you, so that one day you'd have the nerve to come question me! You're garbage and you have no right to ask me anything, you bastard!

And it started.

When Helena went into a rage, the target wasn't just Kim. It was everything around. If there was nothing close, she went to the thing. Walked through the house grabbing, throwing, breaking. Kitchen glasses exploding against the wall. Her screaming and cursing.

Kim was used to it.

His only regret was having cleaned the house earlier.

He knew the name for it. One of the many illnesses his mother carried untreated. The bipolar disorder sent her into those extremes. And he, no longer a child, knew what to do: stay calm and wait for the storm to pass.

He stood up. Went to the kitchen doorway. Stood watching, waiting for a pause. Dodged a few things that came his way. A whole life of dodging flying objects had sharpened his reflexes.

The pause came.

— I came home tired. I'll leave you to your... — A glass flew past his face. — Whoa, that one almost... chores. And I'm going to take a shower. When you calm down, we'll talk. But I already said it and I'll repeat: today you're going to tell me. Or you can keep putting on this show and I'll keep waiting for the answer. See you in a bit, Ms. Helena.

A few more glasses. He dodged all of them and went upstairs.

---

In the shower, he relaxed.

He hoped that when he got out, she'd be calmer. He always found it impressive how fast she changed. Sometimes a single word was enough. He knew that, in part, it wasn't her fault. She couldn't control herself. And it was impossible to convince her to get help.

He got out of the shower. Put on shorts. And heard the crying.

It was always like this. First the fury. Then the sadness. One extreme to the other, like a runaway train.

He went down the stairs slowly, thinking about how to bring up the subject. With each step, the usual insults from below. *Ungrateful boy.* It was the kind of affection he knew.

Helena was on the couch.

Rocking her body back and forth, hand on her face, murmuring something impossible to understand. She seemed on the verge of losing her mind.

Kim sat in the chair and stayed quiet.

She noticed him there. Took her hand from her face.

And broke into a smile. A smile that made Kim uncomfortable, no matter how hard he tried to hide it.

— So you want to know? Everything, right? You want to know everything. From the beginning. From the very beginning. From when you were a baby?

— I wanted to know a little before that, you know. — Kim, confused, worried. — How you met my father. What happened before I was born. Or if he ran off after I was born.

Helena laughed. An uncontrolled laugh. Her eyes almost mad.

— I NEVER MET YOUR FATHER! — She screamed in his face. — I never met him! I don't even know if he's alive or dead! I don't know if he was good or bad! I never saw his face!

---

Kim was floored.

He didn't know if she was messing with him or if it shook her that much. He'd seen many faces of his mother. Never this one.

— I don't understand. — Low. — Did you suffer some kind of abuse? I'm sorry to ask, Mom. But did someone do something to you? You don't know what happened? I need to understand what's going on.

There was a bottle by her leg. She raised the bottle.

Instinctively, Kim took a step back.

She just drank. A long gulp. Wiped her mouth with her hand.

— Yes, boy. I was abused. You can say it. More than once. More than a day, more than a month, more than a year. By more than one man. By more than one animal. That touched me, used me, and destroyed me.

Kim lowered his head. His chest tightened.

Deep down he'd always known something terrible had happened to her. Something that had destroyed her inside. Looking at her now, he realized that, for as long as he could remember, he'd never seen his mother truly smile.

A tear rolled down her face.

— I don't know. I don't know. Maybe your father abused me too. I just don't know. I only know one thing. When I was very little, too little to remember, I must have been kidnapped. And they took me to a place. All I remember is like I was born inside that stinking room. No window. A lamp almost going out. A bucket to do my business. A mattress on the floor. And every night they came. They bathed me. Dressed me up. And took me to different places. To be sold.

She drank again. Her voice almost fading.

Kim didn't know what to say. Rage and sadness rising together.

---

— Then, at some point, when I was twelve... — She laughed with no humor at all. — And I only knew my age because in there the price changed according to age. When I was twelve, they put you in my room.

Kim looked up.

— You were just a baby. Looked like you'd just been born. That's when I thought maybe they'd done the same thing to me. Stolen me from a hospital. Or I was the daughter of some other woman they had in there. Was that how they kept the business going? I don't know. I never knew.

She gripped the bottle.

— I'd already seen boys in there. I knew what was going to happen to you. It was what happened to me and everyone else there. And I was tired. That night they got me ready. And got you ready. You were just a baby, and someone had already paid for you. The whole night. A lot of times they didn't even want anything else. They just wanted to hear a child cry while they hit.

She turned her face. Drank. Breathed. Drank more. When she turned back, she was biting her lips, hands pressed together like in prayer. Tears streaming down uncontrollably, eyes red, swollen.

— You wanted the truth, didn't you? You always thought I was a horrible mother, right? Well, know this. I'm not your mother.

Silence fell over the room.

— I don't even know who your father is. I don't even know who you are. But that night something stirred in me. I looked at you and knew I wasn't going to let them do to you what they did to me. I wasn't going to give them that pleasure. So that night I ran. Jumped out of the car and ran. And I made it. But it was just the two of us in the world. A twelve-year-old girl and a newborn baby.

She looked at him.

— Do you know, Kim, what a twelve-year-old child does to survive in a place like this, with a baby to raise?

---

She stood up. The anger returning, mixed with tears.

— It's a question, boy! Didn't you hear me? Do you know what a twelve-year-old child does to survive with a baby and no adult around?

Kim raised his head a little. Eyes red. Tears falling. Fists closed in front of his mouth.

He shook his head no.

On his face, disgust. Not at her. At the world that had done that.

Helena laughed. Flipped him off. Cursed. Drank.

— Today you should understand why I put you to work when you were twelve. For a girl like me, who knew nothing about the world, the only thing I'd learned was to serve despicable people. And even though it was forbidden, I did what I needed to survive. To feed you. To buy milk. So we wouldn't live on the street. You know what I did? I prostituted myself until you turned six. From twelve to eighteen. At least then it was my choice. To make sure you survived. Only at eighteen did the government let me work.

Kim listened consumed. Anger. Tears. Thinking about the life she'd lived, and the chaos that was his life.

She had more than enough reason for everything she was. What hurt him most was the powerlessness. Not being able to get to whoever destroyed her life.

The bottle was already half empty. She drank the rest until there was nothing left. Sat down. You could see the alcohol rising. Lit a cigarette. Kim lit his.

— I know this story is getting boring. So I'll sum up the end. At eighteen I got my first job. And the bosses were always sons of bitches. I never had luck with anything. I think I was always a big whore, and they could tell. So, to keep the jobs, for six more years I was the mistress of many men. — She blew out smoke. — When you turned twelve, I was tired. Of this shitty life. Of everything I'd done for you. Of trying to be something. I never had anything. Never had happiness. Never had pleasure. So I decided that now you were going to pay me back at least the twelve years I raised you.

She looked at him.

— I always told you until eighteen. The count is different. Twelve for twelve. You're on the fifth. In seven years we're even. Then you do whatever you want. I swear I won't ask for another cent. But until then you owe me a debt. And if you're man enough, you'll pay.

---

Kim slowly lifted his head.

— I understand. You're right. I still owe you seven years. Don't worry. I'll pay each one of them. And much more. If needed, until the last day of your life I'll make sure you lack nothing. No matter what happens.

He'd never felt that way before. Didn't even know he could.

Helena set the empty bottle on the floor. Threw herself back on the couch, tears falling, hands trembling.

— I know now that you know I'm not your mother, things will change. I just hope you don't see me as someone who kidnapped you. Or think I'm capable of making up a story like that. But, being really honest, I don't care what you think. I just saved you. I don't even know why. I only know it was easier to do nothing.

Kim slowly came close and touched her hand.

Lightly.

He realized he didn't remember the last time he'd touched his mother. Hugged her, kissed her. Maybe never. But for some reason he knew he needed to. He held her hand.

— Ms. Helena. No matter what they say. No matter what I do. No matter your past, how we met, or everything we've been through. You always were and always will be my mother. That's how I see you and always will. I will always respect you as my mother. Always love you as my mother. And I'll do my best for you to be well. As my mother.

---

She broke down.

A huge, endless crying. Kim stayed by her side, watching every tear fall, until she seemed empty.

— Kim. I was always afraid to tell you. Afraid you wouldn't believe me. That you'd think I was a liar because of everything I did. I can't live like this anymore. Can't get a job anymore. Can't get involved with men anymore. That's why I needed you. Sorry for dumping a responsibility this size on your shoulders. But I can't anymore. That's the truth. I only feel pain, fear, despair. Rage, hatred, agony. The only time I'm happy is drunk. And that's why I do everything to stay that way every day.

Kim smiled.

And hugged his mother. Tight. Like he'd never hugged before.

She cried again on his shoulder. He ran his hand over her head. And spoke softly, in her ear.

— Mom. — He laughed at himself. — It's okay. Don't worry. You have the right to do whatever you want with your life. But I miss one thing. Actually, I don't even know if I miss it, because I don't remember ever seeing a smile from you. Seeing your mouth lift even once. I don't remember looking into your eyes and seeing a spark of happiness. You have every right to do what you need to be happy. — Pause. — But, of course, without going around destroying other people's property.

Helena laughed.

Kim stood still. It was the first time he'd heard a laugh come out of his mother's mouth.

She kept laughing, now with another tear. A tear she'd never cried before.

— I'm terrible, actually. I'm a terrible mother, a terrible woman, a horrible human being in everything I do. But it was really good... it was really good to hear you call me Mom again. After so long. — She sniffled. — And yes, you dummy, I did smile at you. Back then you were a beautiful, cute little baby. Not this brawling brute you've become.

It was Kim's turn to smile. He never imagined he'd laugh at an insult from his mother.

---

— Hel... err... Mom. — He. — I don't know if you noticed, but I'm going to have to clean the house again, right? It's all kind of messy. I wanted to leave everything in order before bed and before you go out clubbing. Because I know that when you come back, you'll come back with those friends of yours, that way. And I like to sleep with the house tidy. — Pause. — Thank you for everything you told me. For your honesty. I'm grateful you took care of me, saved me, didn't give up on me. And for being my mother. But I need to clean the house.

Helena let go of him. Face swollen from crying. His too. The two smiling at each other.

And then she said something Kim had never heard come out of her mouth.

— Come on, you little fucker. I'll help you tidy up. After all, I'm a little bit to blame for this mess.

And laughed heartily, looking at her son.

Kim tried to keep a straight face.

— Look, Ms. Helena. Not for nothing, but you are definitely the one and only person responsible for this mess. And that's that.

She shoved him, laughing. And started picking up the mess with him.

*So strange.* Kim, picking up shards of glass. *It's like she needed to say this somehow. She seems like a different person.*

---

When they finished, it was already late.

— I'm going upstairs, I have homework. — Kim. — When you come back with your friends, as I always ask, if you can, don't make too much noise. Don't turn the music up loud. So I can sleep a little and make it through class tomorrow.

— Okay, son. You can rest. We won't make noise.

— Good night, Mom. — With a smile. — Be careful out there.

— I'll try. But I'm not promising anything.

— I'll transfer the money to you.

Kim laughed and went upstairs.

Helena watched her son go up. Heard the bedroom door close. And kept thinking about how, despite everything, that boy had become a man. He wasn't perfect. She wasn't his blood mother. And had never been a great mother.

Even so, he was the best son anyone could have.

---

Kim took off his clothes and lay down.

Body tired. Breath light.

A huge wall that had always existed between him and his mother had fallen.

He didn't know if things would stay the same or change.

But he knew that night he was going to sleep well.
