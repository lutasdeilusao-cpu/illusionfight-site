# CHAPTER 14 — Private Room

The square was two blocks from the school.

A peeling cement bench, a tree that gave more shade than fruit, a water fountain that hadn't worked since forever. At that hour there was nobody. Just a pigeon and the three of them.

— So, man. — Jack. — Being real with you, it's time we got to know a little of your power. I thought about it all day. I'm dying to finally fight you inside LDI.

Kim made a confused face.

— But I don't want to fight you. Why would I fight you?

— It's not going to be an official fight, you asshole. I'm talking about us training together. Inside the game.

— Oh, okay then. That's fine. And how do we train in this game?

— Usually we train in a public room. — Nina. — Overcrowded. You get challenged all the time, people getting in the way, some jerks trying to show how strong they think they are. But now me, you, and Jack can use a private training room. It'll make it way easier for us to get ready for the next fights.

Kim got excited. Then his smile fell.

— Guys, I completely forgot something. I got all hyped about all this, but I have to work.

Jack laughed.

— No, you don't.

— Yes, I do.

— No, you don't.

— Yes, I do.

— You dumbass. I'm telling you, you don't. We have a job tonight. It'll pay two hundred each. That's good.

Kim relaxed.

— Oh, in that case, fine. Sorry, Nina, for interrupting. Go on. How do we open this private room? It sounds really cool.

Nina smiled.

— So, kid. Like I was saying. Now we can create a private room. Let's do it. And start your training.

---

Jack already had his SBI in his hand. Nina took hers out of her bag.

Kim stood there looking at the box.

— Open it already. — Jack.

— I'm opening it.

— You've been staring at the box for a minute.

— I'm opening it carefully. Tomorrow this goes back to Ryan.

Jack and Nina exchanged a look that said exactly what they both thought of that.

Kim took off the paper without tearing it. Opened the lid. The SBI was fitted into black foam, thinner than Jack's, lighter, with an unlit line of light running along the edge. He took the device out with both hands, like it was something that could break just from being looked at.

Jack and Nina put theirs on and stood up, ready. Kim copied what the two were doing.

— You're the one ranked highest in SDR out of the three of us. — Jack. — So you open the room. I'm dying to see the private room of someone up there, what resources we'll have. I'll give you a hand creating it, okay?

— How do I open it?

— Dude, this SBI is awesome. — Jack looking at the device in Kim's face. — Now say: NeoGuide, access resources.

The world went dark.

---

— NeoGuide. Access resources.

— Hello, Kim. Welcome back. — The calm voice as always, coming from nowhere. — I detected that you are accessing from a new SBI. I am transferring your settings to this device. Which resource would you like to use?

— That private training room thing.

— Do you want any specific configuration, or is the standard room enough?

— I don't know. The standard one.

— Room configured. Directing.

The floor disappeared.

Kim was falling again.

The arena down below wasn't much different from the first one. Except empty. No stands screaming, nobody looking up. He didn't see a single person while he fell.

*There has to be a way to land without smashing my face.*

— Kim. — NeoGuide inside his head. — There are two entry requests for your private room. Both say they are your friends. Can I approve?

— You can. They're my friends.

The floor arrived while he answered.

He face-planted.

He stayed on all fours on the arena floor with his chin throbbing, his mouth full of a taste of dust that didn't exist.

— Again. — To the floor.

---

The shout came from above.

Kim lifted his head and saw someone plummeting from the sky with open arms, celebrating the fall like someone riding a roller coaster. A dark brown overcoat flapped in the air down to his knees. A red scarf streamed behind him. On his head, a top hat weirdly stuck on, with binoculars attached on top. His whole right arm was golden metal, exposed gears at the joints, nothing high-tech: it looked like a steam engine part. Beard. Mustache. A metal cane in his hand. Steampunk from head to toe.

The stranger landed with knees bent and arms open, stood up slowly, puffed out his chest, and made a two-finger salute on the brim of the top hat.

— What's up. Face-planted again?

It was Jack's voice.

— You have to learn to fall, man. Later we'll figure out a more interesting look for your avatar.

Kim just stared.

— In the stands you had a crown. Shooting fireworks from your back.

— Fan skin. — Jack adjusting his scarf. — To watch I go as king. To fight, this is it.

— You have a beard.

— I have presence.

— You have a top hat with binoculars.

— Kim. Every detail has a function. You'll understand when—

— Wait. — Jack pointed up. — Let's wait for her.

---

Nina fell in silence.

The short hood hissing in the air, the cape down to mid-back, black hair with red streaks that looked like living fire. A red mark crossing her right eye, like a scar someone had drawn with embers. A top with shoulder pads, sleeves in fishnet running down to black fingerless gloves full of spikes. Midriff showing. A black miniskirt loaded with metal pieces at the waist, combat boots up to almost the knee. A katana sheathed at her left hip.

She landed beside them in a pose she had definitely rehearsed somewhere.

— Sorry for the delay, boys. I was picking out some new accessories, taking advantage that they unlocked them. Yesterday I couldn't do anything. What were you talking about?

Jack looked one way. Looked the other.

— Nothing. I was waiting for you to explain LDI to Kim. Now from inside LDI.

— How do I get a look like that? — Kim. First question.

Jack and Nina smiled at the same time.

— Avatar is a very personal thing. — Jack. — I'll teach you, but later. There's the Fashion room, you go in whenever you want and change as many times as you want. It'll be cool to see what you'll put together. Not now. The private room only unlocks two hours a day, so today is training.

— Second question. I remember Thunderbolt also had a weapon. A spear. But he didn't use it against me. You have a cane. She has a sword. Can you use weapons in this game?

— You can. — Nina. — Depends on each player. Thunderbolt uses that spear, but in that fight he probably thought he didn't even need it, against you. Weapons in LDI are free. They're an extension of your power. But there are fighters out there who prefer not to use them.

---

Jack raised a hand with three fingers.

— Three things. One: here everything is in your head. LDI grabs onto the mental strength of whoever plays. If you believe falling from two hundred meters is normal, you fall and land on your feet. If you hesitate, you fall like a sack of cement. That's why you face-plant. You think too much about the ground.

— Two. Every avatar has an elemental. Primary or secondary. Secondary isn't weaker, it just has more combination options. Thunderbolt used electricity, which is secondary of Light. What came out of you in that fight looked like Darkness, which is primary.

— Three. Most important. Everything you know about fighting out there works here. But you have to believe that in here you're as strong as out there. Or stronger. That's not written anywhere. Nobody really knows how LDI works. But it's what I do and it works for me.

— I also think it's all in your head. — Nina. — The stronger I believe I am, the stronger I get in here. And the score comes from that too. The corporation says each person's mental strength decides how much a fight is worth. That's the official explanation. I know it doesn't explain anything.

Kim had the face of someone understanding about half of it.

— Room types. — Jack, changing the subject before his friend locked up. — There are obstacle rooms, rooms with elemental disadvantage, rooms with enemies you've already faced. Today is the simple one, the one you chose. We're going to test your darkness elemental. And since you already showed you have a lot of power in here, me and Nina are going to fight together against you.

— Two against one.

— A fight doesn't have to be one-on-one. It can be two against one, ten against one, two against two. Any format, as long as everyone agrees.

— You can set it at the start of the fight, or before entering, and then just confirm. — Nina.

— And it doesn't have to be only about who knocks who down. There are fights by time, by endurance, by objective, battle royale. — Jack opened his arms. — That's why this game is what it is. But your thing, we already know, is one-on-one.

---

— Before. — Kim. — There's something wrong with me.

The two stopped.

— I feel heavy. My whole body hurts. Like I got beat up yesterday.

Jack made the exact face of someone who forgot to mention something important.

— Recovery time. I forgot to tell you.

— You forgot to tell me? What does that have to do with my body right now, Jack, you son of a bitch?

Nina laughed.

— Calm down, Kim. Calm down. After every fight you need to recover. No matter where you are in the SDR. You came out of that fight destroyed. Given the damage, about four days. Ninety-six hours.

— And each of those days you have to log in and stay still for at least five minutes, which is when the system heals the avatar. — Jack. — There's faster healing and slower healing, depends on the fight. If you enter a fight before, you go in with what you recovered up to that point. In your case, almost nothing. Little power, little speed, little strength. It's like fighting after taking a beating the day before.

— That's exactly how I feel.

— Calm down. There's a little trick. At least for now. — Jack looked up. — NeoGuide. Instant recovery for the room.

— Approach the Recovery Circle, which will now be activated across the entire arena. Good recovery.

Green circles lit up on the arena floor. Jack and Nina walked to them with the ease of someone who had done it a hundred times. Kim stayed still until Jack nodded.

Bands of green light rose from the floor around him, spinning like cold flames. It wasn't hot or electric. It was like pressure distributed over his whole body at once, and wherever it passed, the weight left in layers.

— Recovery complete. All members of this room have been restored. Shutting down.

Kim looked at his hands. Closed them. Opened them. Jumped once. Twice. Jumped higher than he should and landed effortlessly.

— I feel great.

— Then let's go. — Nina.

— Only if it's right now.
