# CHAPTER 8 — The Scorpion Strike

Kim passed the pack.

Nina pulled out a cigarette, put it in her mouth and held out her open hand. He understood and gave her the lighter too. She lit it, gave it back, blew smoke upward.

Jack took advantage of the pack being out of the pocket and grabbed one too.

— Seems you two actually know how to fight. — Nina.

— A little something. — Jack, smiling. — About how to smash people's faces in.

Kim shrugged.

Nina flipped one of the knocked-out goons on the floor with the tip of her shoe. Looked at his face. His clothes.

— These guys aren't even from this school.

— They're not. — Kim. — In this school anyone gets in, whenever they want. Everything here can be bought. Just need the right price.

— And it's normal for rich boys to use daddy's money to pay goons to come after us. — Jack. — It's already part of the routine.

Kim looked at her with the face of someone who has no patience left.

— Nobody usually follows us. So I ask again. Why are you here?

Nina rolled her eyes. Jack smiled at that.

— I like watching fights. — She let the smoke out slowly. — When I saw the size of the mess, I thought you two were going to take a hell of a beating. And I wanted to see it. You were so cocky in class it would've been beautiful to see your faces broken after all that swagger.

Pause.

— Okay. I admit it. I underestimated you.

---

Jack got up laughing and walked over to her.

Kim went the other way. Found a crate, sat down, took his phone out of his pocket and opened his banking app. He wanted to see if Freddy had paid.

— So? — Jack, stopping in front of Nina. — Did we fight well enough for royalty's entertainment?

Nina lowered her face a little. For the first time that day, the bored expression was gone. She raised her hand slowly and ran it over Jack's cheek.

— Don't flatter yourself, sweetheart. To leave me satisfied you'll have to do a lot more than that.

Her hand slid from his cheek to his shoulder.

And pushed.

A light shove, at the exact right spot. Jack, all melted, landed on his ass.

Kim raised his head in time to see his friend falling.

When he looked back at his own hand, the phone wasn't there anymore.

Nina was two steps behind, hopping, shifting her weight from one foot to the other, his phone in her hand, smiling at both of them.

Kim stared at his empty hand. Jack, sitting on the floor, stared at the annex ceiling not understanding how he'd ended up there.

— Alright. — Kim, getting up slowly. — Got it. You're a little quick. But that phone is mine, and I'm going to be really pissed if anything happens to it.

Jack laughed, brushing off his shoulder.

— Seriously, you got me. I let my guard down. Look, I think you're cute and I'm loving this attitude, but me and Kim have a rule about opponents...

— What? — Nina cut in. — You don't hit women? Scared?

---

This time it was her who got caught by surprise.

Kim launched from his spot with his hands on the ground.

On all fours. Like an animal. Low torso, close to the ground, almost not rising. He crossed the annex in a blur and stopped right beneath her.

They looked at each other. Her from above. Him crouched, his eyes not on her. On the phone.

She thought about moving her hand.

Thought.

He had already risen and already had the phone. In the same motion he tossed it to Jack, who jumped up from the floor, caught it in the air and landed on his knees, sitting on his own feet, as if rehearsed.

Nina stepped back. Then another step.

The two seemed like animals. Attack without warning, fast, precise. More precise than many professional fighters she knew. And worse: in sync. One knew where the other was going to be before the other got there.

Kim turned to her suddenly.

She felt it. A wave of anger coming from him, almost physical, hitting her chest.

Then he cracked a half smile. And walked slowly over to Jack, who was still crouched holding the phone.

— I was checking if that piece of shit paid me. Then she grabbed the phone and now I'll have to open the banking app again.

— No problem, I'll check. — Jack, raising the screen. — Show your face for the ID.

Kim looked at the screen. The phone unlocked.

— Send half to yourself.

— Done.

---

Nina stood still in the middle of the annex.

The two had forgotten she existed. Simply forgotten.

— Fine. — She threw the cigarette on the ground and stepped on it. — Now I'm angry and I need to take it out on something. And since I saw you two can handle it, I won't need to hold back. Which one of you goes first?

Kim looked at Jack. *Ask this girl what she wants.*

Jack looked at Kim. *Let it ride. It's funny.*

— It's Nina, right? — Jack.

She nodded.

— So, Nina. Like I was going to tell you before I got interrupted: me and Kim have a rule about opponents. It can be a kid or an old person. Woman or man. Rich boy or kid from the hood. We hit mercilessly either way. — He smiled. — The punch has no prejudice. Here it's all about equality.

Nina laughed. A laugh of respect. Those two were crazy in a way she'd never seen.

She took a deep breath. The anger had gone away. Something else took its place.

— Then I want to fight one of you. Choose who. And I'll be nice and warn you: if you underestimate me because I'm a woman, you're going to get beaten badly. — Pause. — And if you're wondering why I'm picking a fight, it's simple. I like fighting strong guys.

Kim nudged Jack's shoulder.

Jack understood. Went back to the phone.

Kim walked over to her and cracked his neck.

— Don't worry. Unlike Jack, I won't go easy on you.

Jack raised his head with a disapproving look.

— But this isn't going to be a brawl. — Kim. — We have no reason for that. It'll just be a fight. No judge. No points. No rules.

---

Nina raised her arms slowly and stepped into range.

Her stance gave it away. High guard, weight on the back foot, chin tucked. Muay thai, at the very least.

— Agreed. It's not a brawl. — She. — It's a level check. It helps improve my professional fighter skills. From what I saw, you're good enough to handle those troublemakers. But are you good enough for me?

Kim rolled his shoulder, stretching. It was his third fight in a row that day.

— Not that I owe you an explanation, but...

She didn't wait for him to finish.

One leap and the distance vanished. The shin coming in high, straight for the neck.

Kim was caught a little off guard. His instinct was to pull his head back, and pulling his head back would leave his neck even more exposed.

So he did the opposite.

He tilted his head and headbutted her shin, hard.

Nina's leg recoiled and hit the floor. The perfect strike had turned to pain. She switched her stance to protect the hurt shin and took distance.

Kim narrowed his eyes. A small cut had opened on his forehead.

— Neither me nor Jack was fighting those guys seriously. And honestly, I'm pretty wrecked from everything that happened today. — He wiped the blood with the back of his hand. — But let's go. It'll be fun beating a professional.

Jack smiled.

He knew his friend was weak. He had noticed the second the LDI match ended: Kim was out of energy, no matter how much he disguised it. And even so Jack was sure, without a shadow of a doubt, that Kim wasn't going to lose.

No matter the situation.

---

Nina came pressing forward.

Jab. Cross. Jab. Fast, straight, and they seemed heavy. Kim dodging by a hair, always by a hair.

He spun behind a column. Her punch went straight into the concrete.

She didn't even stop.

Kept coming. And in the middle of the sequence she smiled, because she had seen something. She threw her shoulders to one side.

The knee came up from the other side.

It caught Kim square on the chin. His head snapped back. His legs wobbled.

— Now I get it. — Nina, smiling. — You're using my shoulders to guess where each strike is coming from.

She didn't stop talking to hit. She hit while talking. The stretched foot, front kick, in the middle of his chest.

Kim flew back and landed on some old boxes and moldy books. A cloud of dust rose. He stayed on the ground, coughing.

*That one hurt.* Jack, from afar. *You're screwed, Kim. Should have let me fight her.*

But he knew his friend was going to get up.

— So, sweetheart? — Nina. — Isn't it time to call mommy?

Kim got up coughing.

He wasn't angry. Not impatient either. As crazy as it was, you could see a half smile on his face.

He was enjoying it.

— I don't think so. — Cough. — She's probably sleeping — cough, cough — the sleep of the drunk right now.

---

He went back at her with low arms.

No guard at all. When he got close enough, he just threw his shoulders. To one side. To the other. Feinting.

And Nina defended against each one. Strikes that didn't exist, she defended them. By instinct.

Kim opened a full smile.

She got irritated. Tried the feint again, shoulder one way, knee the other.

Only this time he saw the knee coming.

Both hands, which were low, came down on top of her knee. He put his full weight on it and rose. His left leg planted, and his right came from behind, from his back, over the top, curved.

Like a scorpion's tail.

Nina's leg couldn't handle the weight. It gave out. And when it gave out, Kim threw himself to the ground, and the momentum of the fall brought his right foot straight into her chest.

By pure instinct she still crossed her arms in front.

It didn't help. The impact threw Nina back anyway.

Jack fell on his back laughing so hard.

— It's been a while since we've seen the scorpion strike, Master!

Kim looked at him, distracted, and changed his voice. Wise old man voice from a movie.

— The mighty scorpion strike must only be used in times of need, little grasshopper.

Jack clasped his hands in front of his chest, like praying.

— Yes, great Master Bear. As always, your rabbit is wise.

---

Nina was on the ground feeling everything at once.

Anger. Excitement. And a happiness that had nothing to do with getting beaten up.

She'd been on the professional muay thai circuit since she was twelve. She had fought women. She had fought men. And she'd never felt like this in a fight.

Kim was very strong. And he still had more to show, she knew. And those two talking in the middle of a fight as if they were on the couch at home... She was learning. Everything they did opened a door she didn't even know existed.

But above all, she wanted to win.

She got up. Dusted off her clothes.

— Enough. Bio class is almost over and I want to watch the next one, Portuguese. I'll finish this fight in the next minute. But it was fun.

Kim shrugged.

— Same here. I don't want to miss the next class either. Time to end it.

---

They both went.

Nina came faster and heavier than before, feinting in the middle of every strike. For the first time Kim felt the real pressure.

And in the middle of it all he spoke.

— Nina. Do you know the only rule of street fighting?

He spun one hundred and eighty degrees to dodge a strike and stopped behind her back.

She didn't stop or hesitate.

— I know. It's that there are no rules.

He spun another one eighty. Three sixty. Facing her again, at close range.

Nina saw the opening. She twisted her body back and down, loading a furious hook.

And then something flew in front of her eyes.

A shoe.

She didn't understand where it had come from. Looked down. It was Kim's shoe. In the spin, he had kicked his own shoe off his foot, straight at her face.

It was half a second. That half second decided the fight.

She saw his bent leg and read a kick. She did what she had learned her whole life: closed her bent left arm to protect that side and squatted slightly. The hook stayed loaded. She let it go.

Kim dodged easily and stepped on her right foot. Pinned it.

Nina reacted like lightning and twisted her body to face him, but the pinned foot didn't twist with her. She ended up with her back to him, rising on the momentum of the hook, her left arm still guarding a kick that never came.

Completely open.

Kim's elbow went into her chin without him even looking. He was facing away.

The impact, combined with the momentum of her own hook, lifted Nina off the ground. She flew backward.

And behind her there was old furniture. Rusty iron bar. Pointy end up.

---

She didn't get there.

Jack was behind her. Caught her in the air.

A few milliseconds before he had seen: Kim was going to end the fight on the next move. And he had also seen what was behind Nina. He started running before the strike landed.

Kim, in the middle of the elbow, saw Jack pass in the corner of his eye. And held the elbow for an instant. The exact time for Jack to arrive.

— Go fuck yourself, you crazy bastard! — Jack, with Nina in his arms. — You wanted to hurt the girl?

Kim turned smiling.

— Stop the drama. I held the elbow. Gave you time to catch her.

— I saw the world in slow motion! If she'd fallen back there, it was over. It would've ruined everything!

Kim kept smiling. He didn't quite know why. Maybe because of the fight. Maybe because of Jack's desperation.

He knew what was behind her. If Jack hadn't moved, he wouldn't have finished the strike. But Jack had moved.

And the fight had been good. Good enough to get out of his head, for a few minutes, the thing that had been pounding since the LDI match ended.

Then the two saw Nina's arm rise.

Thumb up. Blood running from her mouth. Her pretty face half ruined.

And smiling.

— You two are completely insane!

Kim and Jack laughed. She was liking the fight with that face.

---

When Nina managed to stand, Kim crossed his arms.

— So, girl, what's your deal? What do you want with us, besides fighting?

She let out a loud laugh.

— Nothing. Seriously. I found you two really different from the people at the schools I've been through. I got curious and wanted to see what would happen. That's all. I swear.

Jack dusted off his knees.

— Okay, the talk is good, but it's time to get back to class.

— Jack's right. — Kim. — If we don't go now, we'll be late.

The two grabbed their clothes, put their uniforms back on, and the three went back to the main building.

---

And, by the looks of it, Nina was joining the group.
