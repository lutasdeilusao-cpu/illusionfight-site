# CHAPTER 24 — A Series for the First Time

The Duber smelled of pine air freshener and hidden cigarette smoke.

Kim sat behind the driver and leaned his head against the window. The cut on his forehead throbbed to the rhythm of the street. He'd wiped his face with his T-shirt before getting in the car, but the driver glanced at him in the rearview mirror twice and decided it wasn't his business.

Today he wasn't going to sell candy.

He was working at night. If he took the buses now, he'd get home too late and lose the time to lie down, shower, and get ready. For the first time in a long time, he'd taken a car. Because of the thousand DIX from Freddy and two hundred he hadn't even received yet.

Then he remembered he might find his mother at home.

He sat with that.

That morning she'd made coffee. She'd danced in the kitchen. She'd hugged him in the hallway. And he didn't know what to expect from the afternoon. With Helena you never could tell. Sometimes the calm was just the time the storm took to gather strength.

*Since I got into this shit game, my life's been different.*

There was a new person hanging around with them. That wasn't bad. Actually, it was good. Good in a way he wasn't going to admit out loud. But it was different.

He'd found out something about his father. Or he hadn't. Because the crazy old man who talked to him inside the game didn't know how to talk to a human being. *Your daddy.* For God's sake.

There was a video of him going around on social media that he didn't even really know how it worked. That worried him. He was afraid he wouldn't be able to eat in peace in the mall food court anymore. Even though he almost never went to the mall. Jack was the one who took him.

And for the first time in his life, he'd asked his mother about the past. And found out way more than he wanted.

Somehow, he felt that all of that was connected to the mysterious man. Helena's room. The old man with the staff. The Primordial Blood. Someone behind him. Someone paying to take pictures.

And there was one more thing. A thing deep down that he felt every time he closed his eyes and remembered the arena. Something strong. Dark. Hungry.

He didn't want to think about that.

That was going to torment him very soon.

---

Lost in that, he almost missed his stop.

He paid, got out, walked the stretch of street to his front door. He kept his hand on the doorknob a second longer than he needed.

He opened it.

The house was clean.

Not the morning kind of clean, the kind where you want it to look clean. Actually clean. Dishes washed. Floor swept. Yesterday's shards were gone. The ashtray clean.

Helena was on the couch.

Legs tucked under her, remote in hand, the Cineflix screen glowing on the wall. She turned her head when she heard the door.

— Oh. You're home. Good. — She looked at his forehead and decided not to ask. — No more work today?

— Not today, Mom. Only at night. I came to rest, take a shower, and put on decent clothes. I'm doing security at a club.

— Ah, okay. Good. Rest. Shower. — She stood up. — I made lunch. If you want, I'll heat it up while you shower. Then you can eat. And if you're not going to sleep, we can watch something together. I'm watching a really good series. Money Heist.

Kim stood there in the doorway.

He had never sat down to watch anything with his mother. Never. Not even when he was little. In his memory, the TV in that house was something that stayed on while Helena slept on the couch.

— I'd like that. — He heard his own voice say. — If you heat up the food, that'll be great. After my shower we'll watch that Money Heist thing.

Helena gave a small smile.

He went up the stairs.

She went to the kitchen.

---

The hot water stripped away the dust from the park, the dried blood from his forehead, the smell of the woods.

Kim stayed a while with his hands on the wall, head down, water running down.

Yesterday he'd stood under that shower afraid to know. Today he was afraid of something else. Afraid of getting used to it.

He came down in clean shorts and a T-shirt. The table was set. Just one plate. Rice, beans, a piece of meat, salad.

He sat. He ate.

The food wasn't good. The rice was a little overcooked. The beans were unsalted. The meat was tough.

It was the best food he'd ever eaten in his life.

It had been almost five years since Helena had made lunch. That morning's coffee had been the first. This was the second. Twice in the same day, after five years of none.

Helena sat on the other side of the table with a glass of soda in her hand. Not beer. He noticed and didn't comment.

— So. — She said. — How was your day?

And Kim told her.

He didn't know why. He never told anything. But he told. Walter in the hallway. Ryan with the box. The training room, the axe, Nina cut in half, Jack dancing on top of his body. And the park fight. He told the park fight laughing, the details, the face of the pipe guy when he didn't fall, Roxy taking her shirt off, Jack showing up with the little scratch on his neck.

Helena listened to all of it.

She didn't approve. He could see it in the way she squeezed the glass. But she didn't say anything.

She smiled.

---

After lunch, the two of them went to the living room.

They sat on the same couch. Each on one end. Both awkward, both not quite sure where to put their hands. Helena pressed play.

Within five minutes Kim had forgotten he felt awkward.

The series was good. A bunch of people with city names robbing the mint. A professor who planned everything. People who couldn't trust each other and trusted each other anyway.

He wasn't used to watching anything. His routine was school, work, home, food, studying. The last time he'd watched something just to watch was before he was twelve. Before the debt. That's why he always felt a little left out when Jack talked about movies, games, music. He didn't know any of that stuff. He didn't have time.

At the end of the episode, he stared at the black screen.

— I liked it. — Kim said. — I liked it a lot. I wanted to keep going. But I need to sleep a little before meeting Jack.

Helena kept looking at the screen too.

— Then I won't keep going. — She said. — I'll only keep going if it's with you.

Kim didn't answer.

There was no answer to that.

---

He went up to his room. Lay on his side, staring at the wall.

He dozed off thinking about how, despite everything, a lot of what was changing was good.

Nina was good.

The game, even with all of it, was interesting.

And that thing downstairs, the couch, the series, the "I'll only keep going if it's with you." That was special. That he would keep.

---

He woke at seven-thirty without an alarm.

He put on the suit. The only one he had. Black, a little short in the sleeves, but pressed, without a wrinkle out of place. He adjusted his tie in the bathroom mirror. The cut on his forehead had closed into a thin line.

He looked like another man.

Before leaving, he went to his mother's room. Helena was asleep, curled on her side, her face calm in a way he didn't remember seeing.

He touched her shoulder lightly.

— Dona Helena. I'm heading out, okay? I'll be back around four, five in the morning. All right?

She opened her eyes slowly.

— All right... — Her voice thick with sleep. — But isn't it kind of hard to work that late and then go straight to school?

Kim smiled.

— It's not the first time. You know that. Don't worry. I can take care of myself. I'll sort everything out and everything will be fine.

She closed her eyes again, a half smile at the corner of her mouth.

— Okay, little man. Take care. See you tomorrow. Later, son.

— Later, Mom.

He closed the bedroom door slowly.

Went down the stairs.

In the living room, the Cineflix screen was frozen on the menu, episode two waiting, the show's cover with those smiling masks.

She hadn't continued it.
