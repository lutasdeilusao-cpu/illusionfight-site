# CHAPTER 9 — The Base

In the hallway there was a buzz. People whispering, looking, pointing their phones.

A girl, Jaqueline, walked a stretch on Kim's left side. She also didn't care about the comments. Same as them.

At the classroom door, the teacher everybody called Romancista was waiting for the class to come in.

— Come on, boys! Come on, girls! Beings and beings, people and people! Today I have a very special lesson for you!

She looked at the three and pointed straight at Nina.

— You're the new student who started today, right?

Nina, her mouth still swollen, made an unfriendly face.

— That's me, teacher.

— Oh, good! Today I'm assigning a project, but it'll be easy for you!

— Great. Really great.

---

Romancista closed the door, all excited, and explained the task. A project on the Enlightenment in Bravara. Analyze the authors of the period and present the conclusions. In groups of up to three.

Kim was also interested. Not in Nina. In the project. Kim was a nerd. A study rat. A real bookworm.

In that room there were no desks like in Máquina's class. There were small workbenches, for two or three. Nina was alone at one. She grabbed her bag and went to theirs.

— The project is for three. I'll do it with you guys.

It wasn't a question.

Jack fixed his hair. Got his charm ready.

— Everything in this life has its price.

And wiggled his eyebrows. Heh.

Nina held his chin with her hand, up close, and looked right into his eyes.

— Don't you notice when you don't have the slightest chance?

They laughed. Kim didn't. Kim was already reading.

---

Jack lasted about five minutes.

— Your beauty won't let me focus on the project.

Nina slammed her hand on the bench. Then grabbed Jack by the collar.

— Jack, you son of a bitch, do this shit. I want to finish soon and go home.

Jack raised both hands. And did it.

The three went back to the texts. The pen scratching the screen, the tap, tap, tap. For the first time that day, nobody was trying to hit anybody's face.

Two math classes with Máquina. Biology with teacher Florista, which they'd had in the annex. And now two in a row with Romancista.

After that, the day ended.

---

The bell rang.

Kim closed the tablet. Stood up. Cracked his neck to the side.

— Finally.

*I really need a beer.*

Jack was already beside him, hitting two imaginary drumsticks in the air.

— Hey! Band practice today, let's go!

— You don't give up, do you? Damn.

— How much was the fight again? Two thousand DIX. Exactly. A thousand yours, a thousand mine. It's already in the account. — Jack smiled. — Means today you don't have to go to the bus. Means today we play. Like we agreed.

— We didn't agree on anything.

— We agreed last month. And the one before. And the one before. You always have to work. Today you don't.

Kim looked ahead. Looked at Jack. Let out air through his nose.

Didn't say yes. Didn't say no either. For Jack, it was the same thing.

---

In the hallway, Nina came two steps behind the two of them, watching that with the face of someone who was understanding something about them without anyone having explained anything.

Jack turned around, walking backwards.

— Today we're going to practice with the band. If you want, you're invited.

Kim stopped in the middle of the hallway.

— You can't invite people to my house.

— Dude, it's our base, you know? — Jack, as if it were obvious. — And she's already one of us. For God's sake, Kim. Stop being an Old Grouch.

— Moss Head, stop busting my balls. — Kim walking again. — We're only doing this shitty little session because I've got the week's money. Otherwise I'd go work and you'd be screwed. Don't go thinking you always get what you want.

Jack didn't answer.

*As if I didn't always get it.*

Nina hadn't said she was going. Hadn't said she wasn't either.

She left through the gate with them.

---

The walk was long and Jack filled all of it.

— Do you already know what song you're going to practice? — Nina.

— Of course I know! — Jack.

— He never knows anything. — Kim.

— And what do you play?

— Lately just the songs Jack writes. — Kim.

Nina looked at Jack.

— You compose?

— Do I compose. — Jack with his hand on his chest. — They're the most incredible songs you'll ever hear in your life. Soon they'll be playing everywhere. I'm going to upload everything to PowWow, it's going to blow up, and you'll be able to say: I was there in the garage when it started.

— He's been saying that for five years. — Kim.

— And for five years it's been true. The world just hasn't noticed yet.

— Do you have the lyrics? The chords?

— I'll send them to you now. — Jack, already with his phone in hand.

Her phone beeped.

---

The street started changing.

Nina noticed before she noticed she was noticing. The sidewalks got more broken. The houses got smaller, stuck to each other, with missing plaster and bars on every window. A skinny dog lying on the curb. A barefoot kid watching the three of them pass. Power lines tangled on the pole like hair nobody combs.

It was a sad street.

She looked at the two.

Neither of them seemed to see it. Kim walked ahead, hands in his pockets, greeting an old man at a gate with a short nod. The two walked that street like someone walking inside their own home.

Jack stayed a step behind, beside her.

He had noticed. Nina had been quiet since halfway down the street.

— Hey. — Without the clown voice. — Don't worry, okay?

She looked at him.

— This here. The street, the garage. It doesn't matter. There's every kind of reality in the world. What matters is what we do with it. — He shrugged. — I know from outside it looks bad. But this world here is a world me and Kim built together. And we're really happy in it. As grumpy as he is, he's happy here. Doing this with him is important.

Nina didn't answer.

— And another thing. — The smile returning. — When we start, pay attention. There's an amazing thing Kim does and he doesn't even realize he does.

— What?

— You'll see.

Nina kept looking at Jack.

*I never imagined two people could have a friendship like this.*

She had never managed to make friends. First because she didn't like the people around her. Then because she never stayed in one place long enough to find someone she liked.

It was strange to see such a beautiful friendship up close. So sincere.

---

The garage was beside Kim's house.

Jack got there first and pulled the iron gate with both hands. Krrraaak. The peeling paint fell in flakes.

— It's a garage, Jack. — Kim.

— Welcome to the band's official studio.

Inside, cracked cement floor, a light bulb hanging from a wire, some cardboard boxes stacked in a corner. A plastic chair with one leg repaired with tape.

And the drums.

Old. The snare torn and patched. One of the toms missing, the empty space in the stand like a tooth that fell out. The cymbals dented at the edges.

On the wall, hung on a nail, a guitar. Old too. The body scratched, one of the tuning pegs different from the others.

Kim took the guitar off the nail.

Nina saw.

She couldn't explain what she had seen. The way he held the guitar neck. The way his shoulders dropped. The Kim who walked through school with a face ready to break someone changed. He seemed like another person. Younger, maybe.

She found it strange. Didn't comment.

— So, where is it? — Jack sitting on the drum stool and spinning a drumstick. — Aren't you going to serve a little something? Whip up some drinks for us, host.

— Not here. If Helena finds alcohol in the garage, she drinks it all and still takes my cigarettes. — Kim, without raising his eyes from the guitar. — Later we buy something and drink at the base.

Jack hit the two drumsticks together.

— So enough talk. What did we come here to do?
