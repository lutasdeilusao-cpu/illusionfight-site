# CHAPTER 9 — The Base

There was a buzz in the hallway. People whispering, looking, pointing their phones.

A girl, Jaqueline, walked a stretch on Kim's left side. She didn't care about the comments either. Just like them.

At the classroom door, the teacher everyone called Romancista waited for the class to file in.

— Come on, boys! Come on, girls! Beings and beings, people and people! Today I have a very special lesson for you all!

She looked at the three of them and pointed straight at Nina.

— You're the new student who started today, right?

Nina, her mouth still swollen, made a don't-mess-with-me face.

— That's me, teacher.

— Oh, great! Today's assignment won't be hard for you!

— Great. That must be really great.

---

Romancista closed the door, all excited, and explained the assignment. A paper on the Enlightenment in Bravara. Analyze the authors of that era and present conclusions. In groups of up to three.

Jack broke into a huge smile and looked at Nina. Perfect chance to get her in the group. And to get closer.

Kim was interested too. Not in Nina. In the paper. Kim was a nerd. A study rat. A real bookworm.

In that room there weren't the desks from Máquina's class. There were small tables, for two or three. Nina was alone at one. She grabbed her bag and went over to theirs.

— So. I spent a long time away from Bravara. I'll need to catch up. Are you two going to help me or leave the poor innocent girl to fend for herself?

Jack fixed his hair. Got his charm ready.

— We can help. But everything in this life has a price.

And wiggled his eyebrows.

Anyone watching wouldn't believe it. Nina laughed and shoved his face away.

— Stop it, you weirdo. That's ridiculous.

They laughed. Then focused on the assignment.

---

Jack's phone buzzed under the table.

He ignored it. It buzzed again. On the third, he slid the phone under the desk and glanced at it.

GearShift LDI forum. A post twelve minutes old with four hundred and eighty-seven replies.

He put his phone away without saying anything.

Beside him, Nina had her head down at her tablet, pretending to jot down something about the Enlightenment. Jack didn't need to look to know she'd seen the same thing.

Kim was the only one of the three paying attention in class.

Two math classes with Máquina. Biology with teacher Florista, which they'd had in the annex. And now two in a row with Romancista.

After that, the day was over.

---

The bell rang.

Kim closed his tablet. Stood up. Cracked his neck to the side.

— Finally.

— Dude. Dude dude dude. — Jack, phone already in hand before he finished standing up.

— What is it?

Jack turned the screen to him.

Kim looked at the numbers. The comments in languages he couldn't read. His own nick written in letters of an alphabet he didn't know, repeated in dozens of posts. The view counter that didn't stop climbing for even a second.

He stared for three seconds.

*I really need a beer.*

---

On the way out, the numbers still hadn't left Kim's head.

The counter that wouldn't stop. The comments in languages he couldn't read. He walked down the hallway with that, passed through the gate where Osvaldo waved without asking anything, and Marelia Street opened up in front of the three with the late-afternoon sun hitting the sidewalk all crooked.

— How much was it again? — Kim to Jack, walking.

— Two thousand DIX. Nice and even. A thousand yours, a thousand mine. It's already in the account.

Kim stopped walking for half a second. Just half a second. Then kept going.

— Then I don't need to go to the bus today.

Jack broke into the smile of someone who'd been waiting for exactly that conclusion.

— You don't need to go to the bus today. — Calculated pause. — And you know what that means.

— No.

— It means today we play. Like we agreed.

— We didn't agree on anything.

— We agreed last month. And the month before. And the one before. You always have to work. Today you don't.

Kim looked ahead. Looked at Jack. Let out air through his nose.

— Fine, Jack. Fine. You win. We play.

---

Jack turned around.

Nina was two steps behind them, watching it with the face of someone figuring out something about the two of them without anyone explaining anything.

— So, Nina, what's up? — Jack walking backward. — Kim and I are gonna get some vodka and some cigarettes and get wasted. Wanna roll with the big man? Come see the great skills of the amazing Jack.

Nina opened her mouth to answer.

And a scream cut through the street.

---

Beside the gate, a boy was on the ground.

Seven around him. All in school uniform. Kicking. The boy, smaller and skinnier than any of them, curled up, hands on his head.

Nina took a step forward.

Jack put his hand on her shoulder.

She looked at him. He shook his head, without taking his eyes off Kim.

Kim was already walking.

He walked through the middle of the seven like someone walking through people waiting for a bus. He crouched next to the boy.

— So, man. You just gonna lie there and let them do whatever they want?

The boy looked at him with a dirt-smudged face.

— I can't do anything. I don't even know what I did. I'm too weak. I can't beat them.

— Who said anything about beating them? — Kim. — You just have to make enough trouble for them to stop busting your balls. If you don't take the initiative for your own change, there's no point waiting for someone to do it for you.

Kim stood up. Started to walk away.

A hand grabbed his shoulder.

— Hey, asshole. — One of the seven. Bigger than the others, chubby, expensive watch on his wrist. — Stay out of our beef. Haven't you seen us around? That weakling disrespected me. I asked him to buy me something and he refused.

Samuel.

Kim was about to turn.

Then he saw, out of the corner of his eye, the boy on the ground starting to get up.

And left Samuel's hand where it was.

— Every day you bust my balls. — The boy, on his feet, shaking. — Every day you beat me. I never did anything to you. I'm tired.

One of the seven laughed.

— What are you talking about, punk? — Samuel let go of Kim and turned to the boy. — Just because this jackass showed up you think you've got nerve? I'm gonna put you back in your place.

His foot came up to kick the boy in the face.

And stopped in midair.

Jack had arrived without anyone seeing. He caught Samuel's kick with his own foot, sole against sole, and stood there, calm, looking at the boy.

— So, you gonna just stand there? I'm not holding his foot all day.

The boy understood.

He slammed a kick into Samuel's legs.

Samuel screamed and went down holding his shin. And he cried. He cried from rage and shame, in front of everyone.

Jack made a *didn't see that coming* face.

Then the other six came.

It didn't last. A few hits from Kim, Jack, and Nina, and the sidewalk was full of uniformed boys groaning.

---

The boy came up to the three when everything calmed down.

— Thanks. — Wiping his nose with his sleeve. — I don't know what I would've done without you.

— What's your name?

— Ryan.

— Ryan. — Kim. — Today you got lucky. Those cowards only go after people who don't fight back. You don't have to win. You just have to give them enough of a headache for them to give up.

Nina looked at Kim and smiled without meaning to.

Those two were nothing like she'd imagined.

And Kim and Jack, looking at her, remembered the way she'd jumped into that fight. Without hesitating. She wasn't like the other girls at that school.

— So? — Jack, as if nothing had happened. — You coming or not?

— Okay. — Nina. — I'll go.

Kim stopped in the middle of the sidewalk.

— Man. You're inviting people to my house without asking permission?

— Dude, it's our base, you know? — Jack, as if it were obvious. — And she's already one of the crew. For God's sake, Kim. Stop being an Old Grump.

— Moss Head, stop busting my balls. — Kim starting to walk again. — We're only doing this stupid little jam session because I've got the week's cash. Otherwise I'd go work and you'd be fucked. Don't go thinking you always get what you want.

Jack didn't answer.

*As if I didn't always get what I want.*

---

The walk was long and Jack filled all of it.

— So, what do you two play? — Nina.

— Lately only the songs Jack writes. — Kim.

Nina looked at Jack.

— You write songs?

— Do I write songs. — Jack with a hand on his chest. — They're the most amazing songs you'll ever hear in your life. Soon they'll be playing everywhere. I'm gonna upload everything to PowWow, it's gonna blow up, and you'll be able to say: I was there in the garage when it started.

— He's been saying that for five years. — Kim.

— And for five years it's been true. The world just hasn't caught on yet.

The street started to change.

Nina noticed before she noticed she was noticing. The sidewalks got more broken. The houses got smaller, stuck together, with plaster missing and bars on every window. A skinny dog lying on the curb. A barefoot child watching the three of them pass. Electrical wire tangled on the pole like hair nobody combs.

It was a sad street.

She looked at the two of them.

Neither of them seemed to see it. Jack kept talking about music, about PowWow, about a show he'd seen in a video. Kim walked with his hands in his pockets, greeting an old man at a gate with a short nod. The two of them walked that street like someone walking inside their own home.

---

The garage was beside Kim's house.

An iron gate with peeling paint. Inside, cracked cement floor, a lightbulb hanging from a wire, some cardboard boxes stacked in a corner. A plastic chair with one leg mended with tape.

And the drum kit.

Old. The bass drum torn and patched. One of the toms missing, the empty space in the mount like a tooth that fell out. The cymbals dented at the edges.

On the wall, hanging from a nail, a guitar. Old too. The body scratched, one of the tuning pegs different from the others.

Kim took the guitar off the nail.

Nina saw.

She couldn't explain what she'd seen. The way he held the guitar neck. The way his shoulders dropped. The Kim who walked around school with the face of someone ready to break somebody changed. He looked like another person. Younger, maybe.

She thought it was strange. She didn't mention it.

— So, where is it? — Jack sitting on the drum stool and spinning a drumstick. — Aren't you gonna serve a little something? Mix us up some drinks, host.

Kim leaned the guitar against the wall.

— Fine. I'll go over to Zé's to get something for us to drink.

And left.

---

The garage went quiet.

Jack kept spinning the drumstick. Nina stayed standing in the middle of the cracked cement, hands in her jacket pockets, looking at the lightbulb, the tape on the chair, the hole in the drum.

Jack stopped the drumstick.

He'd noticed. Nina had been quiet since halfway down the street.

— Hey. — Without the clown voice. — Don't worry, okay?

She looked at him.

— This place. The street, the garage. It doesn't matter. There are all kinds of realities in the world. What matters is what we do with it. — He shrugged. — I know from the outside it looks bad. But this world here is a world Kim and I built together. And we're really happy in it. No matter how grumpy he is, he's happy here. Doing this with him is important.

Nina didn't answer.

— And one more thing. — The smile coming back. — When we start, pay attention. There's this incredible thing Kim does and he doesn't even realize he's doing it.

— What?

— You'll see.

Nina kept looking at Jack.

*I never imagined two people could have a friendship like this.*

She'd never managed to make a friend. First because she didn't like the people around her. Then because she never stayed anywhere long enough to find someone she actually liked. No one had ever built a world with her in a falling-apart garage and called it a base.

It was strange to see such a beautiful friendship up close. So honest.

---

Kim came back with a bag.

A bottle of cheap vodka, two limes, a soda, and a little bag of ice that was already sweating. He set up the cups on top of a cardboard box, cut the lime with Jack's pocketknife, squeezed it with his hand, topped it with soda.

The three took their first sip almost at once.

— Now enough stalling. — Kim. — Tell me exactly what happened in that game after I blacked out. And speaking of which, you owe me a beating for getting me into that mess.

Jack laughed.

— Get real, man. I didn't get you into any mess. You wanted to win the money and you won. It just wasn't easy.

— I know you've wanted me to play this shit for a long time. And you took advantage of the situation.

Nina looked from one to the other. She'd watched the whole fight on her SBI. And she'd heard, in the annex, that Kim didn't remember anything. Now she wanted to know where all that had come from.

Jack took another sip and told it. In order, everything. The silver hair, the black fire, the voice that wasn't Kim's, the roar that brought down the arena, the other guy who came out of the dust, Freddy being eaten by the feet.

— I thought you were possessed, Kim. For real. — Jack looking at his cup. — Then the Narrator asked your name. And you said you were The Primordial. Kim Primordial. The whole arena chanted. And before going back to normal, you said you'd be back.

The garage went quiet.

Nina had stopped drinking.

Kim picked up his cup and downed half of it in one go.

— From then on I remember. — He set his cup on the box. — Hmm. I don't know if I should tell you guys this. But okay. Enough drama. Maybe you can help me.

---

Kim told them about the Pajé.

The dark without a floor. The sound of water dripping in a rhythm that wasn't random. The old man stepping out of the shadows slowly.

— Long white beard. Feather hat. Little round dark glasses. Gold chain with a dollar sign. A golden staff with a blue stone at the tip. And, man, wearing sneakers.

Jack froze.

— Then he came at me with some riddle talk I didn't understand at all. Then he turned to the side and started arguing with someone who wasn't there. Lost the argument. And turned back with a completely different voice: I arrived to slay. I arrived to serve, stylish as always.

Jack opened his mouth. Closed it.

— Said he was Pajé Yawanari, from the Xakaxi tribe. That he was the ten out of ten stuck with protecting all the sacred powers of this big old world. That, like my grandpa, I was a little badass. Grandson of the big badass. — Kim, serious. — And that the problem I was going through was just a flea on a monkey's ass.

Jack went down.

Literally. He slid off the drum stool and landed sitting on the cement, his cup still miraculously in his hand, laughing the laugh that comes from a deep place and doesn't ask permission.

— I'M DEAD. I'm in Disneyland. What a wild trip!

Nina held it together until the part about the imaginary partner. Then she doubled over, hand on her knee, laughing so hard she forgot where she'd set her cup.

— I arrived to serve! — She, breathless. — Stylish as always! Holy shit!

Kim kept a straight face as long as he could. Until he remembered the *oh boy, time to let the monster loose* with that beard, those glasses, that dollar sign on his chest. The laugh came out before he could do anything about it.

Even there, almost dying, he'd already thought: if I make it out of this alive, I'm going to laugh a lot at the way that old man talks.

The three stayed like that for a while. Jack on the floor. Nina doubled over. Kim leaning against the wall with his eyes closed.

---

Jack returned to the drum stool with what dignity he could. He tapped the two drumsticks together.

— Now enough talk. What did we come here for?
