# CHAPTER 11 — The Cat's Leap

Kim filled the glasses again and sat on a cardboard box.

— Now we're talking. — He looked at the two of them. — Explain this shit to me properly. LDI, SBI, SDR, SDA, whatever the hell. Everything.

Jack made a face like he was in for a lot of work. He looked at Nina for help. She said yes with her eyes.

They picked up their glasses.

— Let's start from the beginning. — Jack. — In 2022, a mysterious guy named David founded a company here in Bravara. Yohu. And Yohu invented a virtual reality fighting system. The LDI. Illusion Fights.

He drank. Nina continued where he left off.

— In three months LDI was everywhere. In its first year it sold more than any video game in history. It became as common as a cell phone. Then Yohu started getting into everything. Augmented reality, new products for SBI...

That time it was her turn to drain her glass.

— SBI is the Illusion Battle Simulator. — Jack. — The device. Yohu took the technology and shoved it into everything that exists. Education, medicine, research. It became the most powerful Bravara-based company in the world. — He raised a finger. — But let's get to what matters. Nina, explain SDR to him. I'm going to make another drink.

Nina smiled.

The two of them talked to her like she had always been there. She had never had that.

---

— SDR is the ranking system. — she said. — It puts you in a queue with everyone who plays LDI in the world. Each fight gives you points. Nobody really knows how much a win is worth. Yohu says it depends on the energy spent in the fight and the mental strength of the fighters. In theory, a balanced fight gives more points. But LDI doesn't have levels. So you never know if you're fighting someone stronger, equal, or weaker.

Jack came back with a full glass and a huge smile.

— And this is where we pull the cat's trick. To get into the rank, you have to be at least at position one million. There are more than eight billion people in the world. At least three billion playing LDI. Getting into the top one million isn't easy. But if you do, you already have some relevance. Small. But there it is.

— And the rank is divided into bands. — Nina, pouring another round. — From one million to five hundred thousand, those are the least relevant. The least of the least. From five hundred thousand to one hundred thousand, little relevance. From one hundred thousand to fifty thousand, if you work a little, you can already get something. From fifty thousand to ten thousand is the most-watched crowd. They can't make their fights private, so the whole world likes to watch. They're the forty thousand free. — She stopped. — I've said too much. Go on, Jack.

Jack made a *what can you do* face.

— So, man. From there up is where it gets interesting. From ten thousand to one thousand, people start making money with LDI...

Kim's hand clamped onto Jack's arm.

Hard.

— You're saying you can make money with this shit?

Jack laughed. Nodded slowly, up and down.

— You can, man. You can make money with this shit. And it's not a little. We're talking serious cash.

Kim let go of his arm. He drank. For the first time all afternoon, he was really paying attention.

---

— Those between one thousand and ten thousand are the ten thousand specials. — Jack. — And they have access to SDM resources. Monetization. Pay attention to my list, all right.

He counted on his fingers.

— SDA. Betting system. Whoever is watching bets points on the fight. Depending on how many people bet and on whom, you can receive up to ten percent of the winner's points.

— SDD. Donation. Someone in the audience sends you a small donation in exchange for nothing, or a big one in exchange for you saying their name, doing a move, thanking them. The requests come pre-selected by LDI, so nobody asks for anything that messes with the fight.

— SAL. Limited access. You make your fight private, no audience. Or you charge for entry. Or you create an exclusive group and charge an absurd amount for a few people to watch.

— STE. Exclusive training. You teach paying students. Teach moves, become a mentor, a master. Or do a session showing off your powers.

— SDP. Partnership. Either you go after brands the traditional way, or you make yourself available and Yohu connects you with a famous brand. It takes care of everything and you just collect. Your avatar and the arena change to suit the partner in each fight.

— And SVN. NFT sales. Every six months, you and a Yohu designer create ten to thirty exclusive items to sell. There are rewards for buyers, and buyers can resell.

He spread his hands.

— Besides that, since LDI is all over the world, you get a little famous almost instantly. Companies call you for events, media, that kind of thing. Helps the financial side.

Kim had gone from interested to confused in the middle of the list. By the end, his face was closed off.

— So I'm going to get famous if I play this shit? I thought you could be anonymous, I don't know. You know damn well I'm fine with getting rich. But I want to eat in the food court in peace.

Nina smiled.

He was serious. It wasn't posturing. Few people she knew truly wanted a quiet life, without showing off.

Jack put his hand over his face.

— Okay. Later we'll talk about this perception of reality of yours. — He drank. — Continuing. At the end of your first fight, you reached position one thousand two hundred and one in the world ranking. That means, out of everyone playing on the planet, you're getting into the top one thousand. With one fight. I, on the other hand, was the only one who bet on you. With that, plus my points, I went to ten thousand seven hundred seventy-six. In other words, I won...

— And I bet on Kim too. — Nina lowered her glass, already a little tipsy. — Just for the fun of betting on the underdog. I'm at eight thousand seven hundred twenty-four. A cheer for me. Ippee ki yay!

Jack looked at her surprised. He took a deep breath. Took another sip.

— Rephrasing. You're at one thousand two hundred and one. I, who bet, am at ten thousand seven hundred seventy-six. And Nina, who apparently watched in secret and also bet, is at eight thousand seven hundred twenty-four.

Nina was beaming. Jack saw she was getting drunk and laughed. The two of them toasted. Kim, not quite understanding their happiness, toasted too.

— With that, Nina and I became world top with medium relevance. — Jack. — You, man, became world top with high relevance. If you work hard for two or three years, maybe you'll reach the world tournament. The one hundred twenty-eight best LDI fighters in the world. They face each other on David's private island, Davoss. Every four years.

— And with that, some monetization systems already unlock for us. — Nina, her voice already a little slurred. — And for you, Kim: it's from the top one million that you start being tracked. Everyone can find you, see who you are. So you'd better draw up a strategy. A goal. What you want to do with LDI to change your life. — Pause. — Being really honest, it only depends on you.

Jack, already drunk too, nodded his head to all of it.

— So, man. Now one of the resources unlocked for you is changing your nick and avatar. Like the great LDI champion. You can keep your identity a secret.

Kim raised his glass, happy for the first time in the conversation.

— Ah, now I like it. You mean I get to go stealth mode? Now it got interesting. But you guys are going to have to teach me.

Jack hugged him, the alcohol already rising, the voice of a good drunk.

— Of course, my friend! Of course I'll help you customize your avatar and your nick. But for that you need your own SBI. Today we don't worry about that. Tomorrow, after school, I'll figure something out. Any other questions?

Kim knew his friend. He knew this would end in trouble. He smiled, hugged back, and accepted his fate.

As always. For better or worse, the two of them went in headfirst together.

---

— Ohhh. — Nina. — How cute you two are together. At school you look like the biggest troublemakers. Alone, you look like two kids playing. It's really weird hanging out with you.

Kim and Jack started petting each other's heads. *Ohhh.* Laughing. They even exchanged a little kiss on the cheek.

Nina cracked up.

— Oh, oh. If you two weren't so gay, maybe I'd hook up with one of you. Or both.

Jack got interested immediately. He got very close to her face.

— You know, beautiful, I don't like to share. Kim and I have never done that. But maybe, for you, we would.

— No. — Kim protesting with his whole body.

Jack motioned with his hand for him to shut up.

Nina ran her hand over Jack's face, slowly, with a mischievous smile.

— You know, handsome, I'm being sincere when I say I'd hook up with both of you. But I'm afraid you two aren't man enough to handle it. I'm too much woman for you.

— HOOOOLY SHIIIIIT!

Kim's shout echoed through the garage.

Jack lowered his head, defeated. And Kim, who should also have been offended, started laughing. Maybe at her answer. Maybe at the vodka. But he didn't stop.

Jack walked away grumbling.

— Jeez. We were getting along so well. Why all this rudeness? There's a serious lack of love in that heart.

Nina, laughing, tried to get close to pet the poor wounded animal. And he kept moving away. Very, very offended.

---

The rest of the afternoon was nonsense.

Jack telling old fights and stories as he remembered them. Nina telling how she had spent the last few years in Azuma, because of her father's work, which she called consulting, with a pause before the word that Kim registered and didn't comment on. The bottle emptying.

When the street light started turning orange and then vanished, the three of them stood up. A little wobbly.

— Well, guys. — Kim. — The talk is good, but I've got to head out.

— Yeah. — Jack, groggy. — Kim's right. I should already be home. I still have to do some work tonight. I need a shower.

Nina leaned on Jack.

— Look, guys. This afternoon was one of the most fun I remember. But I really have to go. My dad's already texting me.

They picked up their own trash. Called a car for her and waited together on the sidewalk until it arrived.

She got in. Rolled down the window.

— Tomorrow you have an SBI to sort out and a nick to choose. — To Kim. — Think hard about the nick.

— It's going to be Briguento for real.

— And you guys send me the next song. — she said. — With the chords.

The car drove off.

---

Kim and Jack sat on the curb of the sad street, looking at the empty corner.

Jack was still smiling. Out of nowhere, he got serious.

— Man. I didn't want to ask with Nina here, because I didn't know how you'd react. But first, another question. What did you think of her now?

Kim scratched his chin.

— Honestly, she kind of changed my first impression. But she's still under evaluation. — Pause. — I never expected to make another friend in my life, besides you and Alan. But I'm not going to close my heart.

A half smile, almost invisible, appeared on Jack's face.

He was happy to see his friend opening up to new things. LDI. Nina. And he himself felt good beside her. As if he had known her for years.

Then the serious face returned.

— Man. I know we made fun of that trendy old guy a lot. We laughed like crazy. But there was one thing he said that I know got to you. — He looked at Kim. — He said something about your family, didn't he?

Kim looked at Jack, smiling. It was unbelievable how well that guy knew him. You couldn't hide anything.

— You're the man, man. All right. It got to me, yeah. He said that, like my grandfather, I'm a little badass. Grandson of the big badass. — He looked at the street. — My grandfather, Jack. If he knows about my grandfather, he knows about my father. I felt like asking Helena.

Jack scratched his head. He had always known that was a sensitive subject.

— Man, I know you don't like talking about it. So I'm not going to push anything, like I never have. I'll just tell you one thing. Whatever decision you make, whether to talk to your mom or not, to find out who your father is or not... you know I'm here, right?

Kim smiled. Once again, happy to have that friend.

— Yeah, man. Yeah. It's weird for me to talk about that guy. I don't even know who he is, or what happened between him and Helena. And that's it. Just that. That's why I don't like people asking me about something I don't even have a starting point to answer.

---

— You're going to try today. — Jack, without looking at him.

— I am.

— Okay. Text me if you need anything.

— I won't need to.

— I know. Text me anyway.

Jack stood up, dusted off his pants, and walked down the street to his house with the drumsticks in his back pocket.

— Get home alive tomorrow in time for class. — Kim, loud.

Jack raised his hand without turning around.

Kim closed the garage gate.

He stayed for a second with his hand on the cold iron. Inside, in the dark, the guitar hanging on the nail, the bass against the wall, the drum kit with the hole where a tom was missing.

For the first time, the garage seemed too small.
