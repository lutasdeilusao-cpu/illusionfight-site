# CHAPTER 11 — The Trick

After rehearsal, Kim grabbed the bag hidden behind the wall and the three of them headed to the base.

An empty parking lot at the end of the street. A few old abandoned cars — a wheel-less Beetle, a pickup truck with a tree growing out of the engine, a red hatchback with the back door open forever. And what was left of a police station, the sign half painted over. The cars were impounded from back when it was still operating.

Nobody went there. Not the police. Not even Helena.

Kim set up the cups on the hood of the hatchback. Jack cut the lemon with his pocketknife. Vodka, lemon squeezed by hand, soda. Kim sat on the curb.

— Now we're talking. — He looked at the two of them. — Explain this shit to me properly. LDI, SBI, SDR, SDA, whatever the hell. Everything.

Jack made the face of someone about to have a lot of work. He looked at Nina for help. She gave him a tiny nod.

They both picked up their cups.

— Let's start from the beginning. — Jack said. — In 2022, a mysterious guy named David founded a company here in Bravara. Yohu. And Yohu invented a virtual reality fighting system. LDI. Illusion Fights.

He drank. Nina picked up where he left off.

— In three months, LDI was everywhere. In the first year it sold more than any video game in history. It became as common as cell phones. Then Yohu started getting into everything. Augmented reality, new product for SBI…

This time she was the one who drained her cup.

— SBI is Illusion Battle Simulator. — Jack said. — The device. Yohu took the technology and shoved it into everything that exists. Education, medicine, research. It became the most powerful company in Bravara in the world. — He raised a finger. — But let's get to what matters. Nina, explain SDR to him. I'm gonna make another drink.

Nina smiled.

The two of them talked to her like she'd always been there. She'd never had that.

---

— SDR is the ranking system. — she said. — It puts you in a queue with everyone who plays LDI in the world. Each fight gives you points. Nobody really knows how much a win is worth. Yohu says it depends on the energy spent in the fight and the mental strength of whoever's fighting. In theory, a balanced fight gives more points. But LDI has no levels. So you never know if you're fighting someone stronger, equal, or weaker.

Jack came back with a full cup and a huge smile.

— And this is where the trick comes in. To get into the rank, you have to be at least in position one million. There are more than eight billion people in the world. At least three billion playing LDI. Making it into the top one million already isn't easy. But if you do, you already have some relevance. Small. But you have it.

— And the rank is divided into bands. — Nina said, mixing another round. — From one million to five hundred thousand, those are the least relevant. The least of the least. From five hundred thousand to one hundred thousand, little relevance. From one hundred thousand to fifty thousand, if you work a little, you can get something. From fifty thousand to ten thousand is the most watched crowd. They can't lock their fights, so the whole world likes to watch. They're the forty thousand free. — She stopped. — I've talked too much. Go on, Jack.

Jack made a *what can you do* face.

— So, man. From there up is where it gets interesting. From ten thousand to one thousand, people start making money with LDI…

Kim's hand clamped onto Jack's arm.

Hard.

— Are you saying you can make money with this shit?

Jack laughed. Nodded slowly, up and down.

— You can, man. You can make money with this shit. And it's not a little. We're talking serious cash.

Kim let go of his arm. He drank. For the first time all afternoon, he was really paying attention.

---

— Those between one thousand and ten thousand are the ten thousand specials. — Jack said. — And they have SDM resources. Monetization. Pay attention to my list, okay.

He counted on his fingers.

— SDA. Betting system. Whoever's watching bets points on the fight. Depending on how many people bet and on whom, you can receive up to ten percent of the winner's points.

— SDD. Donation. Someone in the audience sends you a small donation for nothing, or a big one in exchange for you saying their name, doing a move, thanking them. The requests already come pre-selected by LDI, so nobody asks for anything that disrupts the fight.

— SAL. Limited access. You close your fight, no audience. Or charge admission. Or create an exclusive group and charge an absurd amount for few people to watch.

— STE. Exclusive training. You teach paying students. Teach moves, become a mentor, a master. Or do sessions showing off your powers.

— SDP. Partnership. Either you chase brands the traditional way, or you make yourself available and Yohu connects you with famous brands. It handles everything and you just receive. Your avatar and the arena change to suit the partner in each fight.

— And SVN. NFT sales. Every six months, you and a Yohu designer create ten to thirty exclusive items to sell. It gives rewards to buyers, and buyers can resell.

He opened his hands.

— Besides that, since LDI is all over the world, you get a little famous almost immediately. Companies call you for events, media, that kind of thing. Helps the financial side.

Kim had gone from interested to confused in the middle of the list. By the end, his face was shut.

— So I'm gonna get famous if I play this shit? I thought you could be anonymous, I don't know. You know very well I'd be fine getting rich. But I want to eat at the food court in peace.

Nina smiled.

He was serious. It wasn't posturing. Few people she knew truly wanted a quiet life, no showing off.

Jack put his hand over his face.

— Okay. We'll talk about your perception of reality later. — He drank. — Continuing. At the end of your first fight, you landed at position one thousand two hundred and one in the world ranking. That means, out of everyone who plays on the planet, you're approaching the top one thousand. With one fight. I, on the other hand, was the only one who bet on you. With that, plus my points, I went to ten thousand seven hundred seventy-six. In other words, I won…

— And I bet on Kim too. — Nina lowered her cup, already a little tipsy. — Just for the fun of betting on the underdog. I'm at eight thousand seven hundred twenty-four. Cheers to me. Ippee ki yay!

Jack looked at her surprised. He took a deep breath. Took another sip.

— Let me rephrase. You're at one thousand two hundred one. I, who bet, am at ten thousand seven hundred seventy-six. And Nina, who apparently watched in secret and also bet, is at eight thousand seven hundred twenty-four.

Nina was beaming. Jack saw she was getting buzzed and laughed. The two of them toasted. Kim, not quite understanding their happiness, toasted too.

— With that, me and Nina became world top of medium relevance. — Jack said. — You, man, became world top of high relevance. If you work hard for two or three years, maybe you'll reach the world tournament. The hundred and twenty-eight best LDI fighters in the world. They face each other on David's private island, Davoss. Every four years.

— And with that, some monetization systems already unlock for us. — Nina said, her voice already a little slurred. — And for you, Kim: it's from top one million that you start being tracked. Everyone can find you, see who you are. So you better come up with a strategy. A goal. What you want to do with LDI to change your life. — Pause. — Being really honest, it only depends on you.

Jack, also buzzed, nodded at everything.

— So, man. Now one of the resources that unlocked for you is changing your nick and avatar. Like the great LDI champion. You can keep your identity secret.

Kim raised his cup, happy for the first time in the conversation.

— Ah, now I liked that. You mean I go stealth mode? Now it got interesting. But you two are gonna have to teach me.

Jack hugged him, the alcohol already climbing, the voice of a happy drunk.

— Of course, my friend! Obviously I'll help you customize your avatar and your nick. But for that you need your own SBI. Today we don't worry about it. Tomorrow, after school, I'll figure it out. Any other questions?

Kim knew his friend. He knew that would lead to trouble. He smiled, hugged back, and accepted his fate.

As always. For better or worse, the two of them went in headfirst together.

---

— Ohhh. — Nina said. — You two are so cute together. At school you look like the biggest troublemakers. Alone, you look like two kids playing. It's really weird hanging out with you.

Kim and Jack started petting each other's heads. *Ohhh.* Laughing. They even exchanged a little kiss on the cheek.

Nina cracked up.

— Oh, oh. If you two weren't so gay, maybe I'd hook up with one of you. Or both.

Jack got interested immediately. He got really close to her face.

— You know, gorgeous, I don't like to share. Me and Kim have never done that. But maybe, for you, we would.

— No. — Kim protested with his whole body.

Jack made a gesture for him to shut up.

Nina ran her hand over Jack's face, slowly, with a mischievous smile.

— You know, handsome, I'm being sincere when I say I'd hook up with both of you. But I'm afraid you two aren't man enough yet to handle the job. I'm too much woman for you.

— HOLYYYY SHIIIIIT!

Kim's shout echoed through the empty parking lot.

Jack lowered his head, defeated. And Kim, who should have been offended too, started laughing. Maybe at her answer. Maybe at the vodka. But he didn't stop.

Jack walked away grumbling.

— Wow. We were getting along so well. What's with all this rudeness? So little love in that heart.

Nina, laughing, tried to get close to pet the poor wounded animal. And he kept backing away. Very, very offended.

---

The rest of the afternoon was nonsense.

Jack telling old fights and stories as they came to him. Nina telling how she'd spent the last few years in Azuma, because of her father's work, which she called consulting, with a pause before the word that Kim registered and didn't comment on. The bottle emptying.

When the streetlight started turning orange and then disappeared, the three of them got up. A little crooked.

— Alright, guys. — Kim said. — The talk's good, but I gotta head out.

— Yeah. — Jack said, dizzy. — Kim's right. I should already be home. I still have to pull a shift tonight. I need a shower.

Nina leaned on Jack.

— Look, guys. This afternoon was one of the most fun I remember. But I really have to go. My dad's already texting me.

They picked up their own trash. Called a car for her and waited together on the sidewalk until it arrived.

She got in. Rolled down the window.

— Tomorrow you have an SBI to get and a nick to choose. — To Kim. — Think hard about the nick.

— It's gonna stay Briguento anyway.

— And you two send me the next song. — She said. — With the chord chart.

The car drove off.

---

Kim and Jack sat on the curb of the sad street, looking at the empty corner.

Jack was still smiling. Out of nowhere, he got serious.

— Man. I didn't want to ask with Nina here, because I didn't know how you'd react. But before, another question. What did you think of her now?

Kim scratched his chin.

— Honestly, she kind of changed my first opinion. But she's under evaluation. — Pause. — I never expected to make friends with anyone else in life, besides you and Alan. But I'm not gonna close my heart off.

A half smile, almost invisible, appeared on Jack's face.

He was happy to see his friend opening up to something new. LDI. Nina. And he himself felt good around her. Like he'd known her for years.

Then the serious face came back.

— Man. I know we made fun of that modern old guy a lot. We laughed our asses off. But there was one thing he said that I know got to you. — He looked at Kim. — He said something about your family, didn't he?

Kim looked at Jack, smiling. It was unbelievable how much that guy knew him. He couldn't hide anything.

— You're the shit, man. Alright. It got to me, yes. He said that, like my grandfather, I'm a little badass. Grandson of the big badass. — He looked at the street. — My grandfather, Jack. If he knows about my grandfather, he knows about my father. It made me want to ask Helena.

Jack scratched his head. He'd always known that was a sensitive subject.

— Man, I know you don't like talking about this. So I'm not gonna push anything, like I never have. I'm just gonna tell you one thing. Whatever decision you make, whether to talk to your mom or not, whether to find out who your father is or not… you know I'm here, right?

Kim smiled. Once again, happy to have that friend.

— Yeah, man. Yeah. For me it's weird talking about that guy. I don't even know who he is, or what happened between him and Helena. And that's it. Just that. That's why I don't like people asking me about something I don't even have a starting point to answer.

---

— You're gonna try today. — Jack said, without looking at him.

— I will.

— Okay. Text me if you need anything.

— I won't need anything.

— I know. Text me anyway.

Jack got up from the curb, dusted off his pants, and walked off down the dark street with the drumsticks in his back pocket.

— Get there alive tomorrow in time for class. — Kim said, loud.

Jack raised his hand without turning around.

Kim gathered the cups, walked home alone, and closed the garage gate.

He stood there a second with his hand on the cold iron. Inside, in the dark, the guitar hanging on the nail, the bass leaning against the wall, the drum set with the hole where a tom was missing.

For the first time, the garage felt too small.
