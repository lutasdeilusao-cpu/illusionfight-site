# CHAPTER 11 — The Trick

The base was an empty parking lot at the end of the street.

Some old dumped cars, a wheel-less Beetle, a pickup with a tree growing out of the hood, a red hatchback with the back door open forever. And what was left of a police station, the sign half painted over. The cars were impounded from back when it was still operating.

No one went there. Not the police. Not even Helena.

Jack was up front, the bag swinging. Thup, thup, his sneakers on the asphalt.

— What a day, huh? — He said, to the sky. — A fight behind the school, Kim debuting in the LDI and a bunch of weird stuff. We made a friend, rehearsed with the band and still stopped a robbery. — He spread his arms. — Feels like someone up there is fucking with our lives just to entertain others. Ever think we might just be comic book characters?

He whistled.

— Who are you talking to, Jack? — Kim said.

— Nobody. Forget it.

He set the bag on the hood of the red hatchback.

Nina came behind, arms crossed. Still had flour in her pink hair.

— Are you two gonna pretend nothing happened?

---

— There were guns, guys! — She said, hands in the air. — Three armed men!

Jack sat on a concrete block and lit a cigarette. Tchk.

— Three badly armed men. That's different.

Kim leaned against the hatchback and opened the vodka. Crick.

— And what if one of them had hit? — Nina said, now to Kim.

— He didn't.

— That's your answer?

— It's the only one I have.

Nina sat on the curb. Jack sat next to her.

— We weren't gonna let the guy get robbed.

— I know. — She looked at her own hand. — But my hand is still shaking.

Kim held out the bottle.

— Then drink, it'll pass. — Jack said.

She drank. A big gulp. Glug.

She handed the bottle back. Her hand still shook. Less.

— Where did you guys learn to fight like that?

— On the street. — Jack said. — Surviving every day.

Kim set up the cups on the hood. Jack cut the lemon with his pocketknife. Vodka, hand-squeezed lemon, soda. Kim sat on the curb, on the other side of Nina.

— Let's drop that talk. — He looked at the two of them. — I want to know everything you know about this LDI shit. LDI, SBI, SDR, SDA, whatever the hell. Everything.

Jack made the face of someone about to do a lot of work. He looked at Nina for help. She nodded with her eyes.

They both grabbed their cups.

— Let's start from the beginning. — Jack said. — In 2022, a mysterious guy named David founds a company here in Bravara. Yohu. And Yohu invents a virtual reality fighting system. The LDI. Illusion Fights.

He drank. Nina picked up where he left off.

— In three months LDI was everywhere. In its first year it sold more than any video game in history. It became as common as cell phones. Then Yohu started getting into everything. Augmented reality, new products for SBI...

This time she drained her cup.

— SBI is Illusion Battle Simulator. — Jack said. — The device. Yohu took the technology and shoved it into everything that exists. Education, medicine, research. It became the most powerful Bravara-based company in the world. — He raised a finger. — But let's get to what matters. Nina, explain SDR to him. I'll fix another drink.

Nina smiled.

The two talked to her like she'd always been there. She'd never had that.

---

— SDR is the ranking system. — She said. — It puts you in a line with everyone who plays LDI in the world. Each fight gives you points. Nobody really knows how much a win is worth. Yohu says it depends on the energy spent in the fight and the mental strength of whoever's fighting. In theory, a balanced fight gives more points. But LDI has no level. So you never know if you're fighting someone stronger, equal, or weaker.

Jack came back with a full cup and a huge smile.

— And here's the trick. To get into the rank, you have to be at least at position one million. There are more than eight billion people in the world. At least three billion play LDI. Getting into the top one million is already not easy. But if you get in, you already have some relevance. Small. But you have it.

— And the rank is divided into bands. — Nina said, making another round. — From one million to five hundred thousand, they're the least relevant. The least of the least. From five hundred thousand to one hundred thousand, little relevance. From one hundred thousand to fifty thousand, if you work a little, you can already get something. From fifty thousand to ten thousand is the most-watched crowd. They can't close their fights, so the whole world likes to watch. They're the forty thousand free. — She stopped. — I've said too much. Go on, Jack.

Jack made a *what can you do* face.

— So, man. From there up is where it gets interesting. From ten thousand to one thousand, people start making money with LDI...

Kim's hand grabbed Jack's arm.

Hard.

— Are you telling me you can make money from this shit?

Jack laughed. Nodded slowly, up and down.

— You can, man. You can make money from this shit. And not a little. We're talking serious cash.

Kim let go of his arm. Drank. For the first time all afternoon, he was paying real attention.

---

— Those between one thousand and ten thousand are the ten thousand specials. — Jack said. — And they have the SDM resources. Monetization. Pay attention to my list, okay.

He counted on his fingers.

— SDA. Betting system. Whoever watches bets points on the fight. Depending on how many people bet and on whom, you can receive up to ten percent of the winner's points.

— SDD. Donation. Someone in the audience sends you a small donation in exchange for nothing, or a big one in exchange for you saying their name, making a move, thanking them. The requests come pre-selected by LDI, so nobody asks for anything that gets in the way of the fight.

— SAL. Limited access. You close your fight, no audience. Or charge admission. Or create an exclusive group and charge an absurd amount for a few people to watch.

— STE. Exclusive training. You teach paying students. Teach moves, become a mentor, master. Or do sessions showing off your powers.

— SDP. Partnership. Either you chase brands the traditional way, or you make yourself available and Yohu connects you with a famous brand. They take care of everything and you just collect. Your avatar and the arena change to serve the partner in each fight.

— And SVN. NFT sales. Every six months, you and a Yohu designer create ten to thirty exclusive items to sell. Gives rewards to buyers, and buyers can resell.

He spread his hands.

— Besides that, since LDI is worldwide, you get a little famous almost immediately. Companies call you for events, media, that kind of thing. Helps the financial side.

Kim had gone from interested to confused in the middle of the list. By the end, his face was closed off.

— So I'm gonna get famous if I play this shit? I thought you could be anonymous, I don't know. You know damn well I'd be fine getting rich. But I want to eat at the food court in peace.

Nina smiled.

He was serious. It wasn't an act. Few people she knew truly wanted a quiet life, without showing off.

Jack put his hand over his face.

— Okay. Later we'll talk about your perception of reality. — He drank. — Continuing. At the end of your first fight, you reached position one thousand two hundred and one in the world ranking. That means, out of everyone who plays on the planet, you're approaching the top one thousand. With one fight. I, on the other hand, was one of the only ones who bet on you. With that, plus my points, I went to ten thousand seven hundred seventy-six. In other words, I won...

— And I bet on Kim too. — Nina lowered her cup, already a little tipsy. — Just for the fun of betting on the underdog. I'm at eight thousand seven hundred twenty-four. Cheers to me. Ippee ki yay!

Jack looked at her surprised. Took a deep breath. Took another sip.

— Rephrasing. You're at one thousand two hundred and one. I, who bet, am at ten thousand seven hundred seventy-six. And Nina, who apparently watched in secret and also bet, is at eight thousand seven hundred twenty-four.

Nina was beaming. Jack saw she was getting drunk and laughed. The two toasted. Kim, not quite understanding their happiness, toasted too.

— With that, me and Nina became top world with medium relevance. — Jack said. — You, man, became top world with high relevance. If you work hard for two or three years, you might reach the world tournament. The one hundred twenty-eight best LDI fighters in the world. They face each other on David's private island, Davoss. Every four years.

— And with that, some monetization systems already unlock for us. — Nina said, her voice a little slurred. — And for you, Kim: it's from the top one million that you start being tracked. Everyone can find you, see who you are. So you'd better draw up a strategy. A goal. What you want to do with LDI to change your life. — Pause. — To be really honest, it only depends on you.

Jack, already drunk too, nodded at everything.

— So, man. Now one of the resources that unlocked for you is changing your nick and avatar. Like the great LDI champion. You can keep your identity a secret.

Kim raised his cup, happy as hell for the first time in the conversation.

— Ah, now I like it. You mean I get stealth mode? Now it got interesting. But you guys are gonna have to teach me.

Jack hugged him, the alcohol already rising, the voice of a happy drunk.

— Of course, my friend! Of course I'll help you customize your avatar and your nick. But for that you need your own SBI. Today we don't worry about that. Tomorrow, after school, I'll figure something out. Any more questions?

Kim knew his friend. He knew that was going to lead to trouble. Smiled, hugged back, and accepted fate.

As always. For better or worse, the two went in headfirst together.

---

— Ohhh. — Nina said. — You guys are so cute together. At school you look like the biggest troublemakers. Alone, you look like two kids playing. It's really weird hanging out with you.

Kim and Jack started petting each other's heads. *Ohhh.* Laughing. They even exchanged a little kiss on the cheek.

Nina cracked up.

— Oh, oh. If you two weren't so gay, maybe I'd hook up with one of you. Or both.

Jack got interested immediately. Got really close to her face.

— You know, beautiful, I don't like to share. Me and Kim never did that. But maybe, for you, we would.

— No. — Kim protested with his whole body.

Jack made a hand gesture for him to shut up.

Nina ran her hand over Jack's face, slowly, with a sly smile.

— You know, handsome, I'm being sincere when I say I'd hook up with you both. But I'm afraid you two aren't man enough yet to handle the job. I'm too much woman for you.

— EEEEEEEEITAAAAAAA HOLY SHIIIIIT!

Kim's shout echoed through the empty parking lot.

Jack lowered his head, defeated. And Kim, who should also have been offended, started laughing. Maybe at her answer. Maybe from the vodka. But he couldn't stop.

Jack walked away grumbling.

— Jeez. We were getting along so well. What's with all this ignorance? So much lack of love in that heart.

Nina, laughing, tried to get close to pet the poor wounded animal. And he kept backing away. Very, very offended.

---

The rest of the night was nonsense.

Jack telling old fights and stories as he remembered them. The bottle emptying.

Until Jack pinched Kim's cheek, laughing, and turned to her.

— And your story, Nina? What is it?

Nina went serious. Swirled the almost empty bottle in her hand.

— I spent the last few years in Azuma. Because of my dad's work. — A pause. — Consulting.

The pause before the word was something Kim registered. And didn't comment.

When the bottle ran out, the three stood up. A little crooked.

— Well, folks. — Kim said. — The talk is good, but I gotta head out.

— Yeah. — Jack said, dizzy. — Kim's right. I should already be home. I still have to pull a shift tonight. I need a shower.

Nina leaned on Jack.

— Look, guys. This afternoon was one of the most fun I can remember. But I really have to go. My dad's already texting me.

They picked up their own trash. Called a car for her and waited together on the sidewalk until it arrived.

She got in. Rolled down the window.

— Tomorrow you have an SBI to get sorted and a nick to choose. — To Kim. — Think hard about the nick.

— It's gonna be Brawler.

— And you guys send me the next song. — She said. — With the chord chart.

The car left.

---

Kim and Jack sat on the curb of the sad street, staring at the empty corner.

Jack was still smiling. Out of nowhere, he went serious.

— So, what do you think of her now?

Kim scratched his chin.

— She changed my first impression. But she's under evaluation. — Pause. — I never thought I'd have another friend besides you. But I'm not gonna close the door.

A half smile, almost invisible, appeared on Jack's face.

He was happy to see his friend opening up to new things. The LDI. Nina. And he himself felt good next to her. Like he'd known her for years.

Then the serious face came back. He stubbed out his cigarette on the ground.

— Kim. I feel like there's something you still haven't told me.

Kim looked at Jack. It was unbelievable how much that guy knew him. There was no hiding anything.

— There is. — He looked at the street. — In the fight. Before I blacked out. There was an old man. A pajé. He talked to me like he knew me.

---

And he told.

The floorless dark. The sound of water dripping in a rhythm that wasn't random. The old man coming out of the shadows slowly.

— Long white beard. Feather hat. Round little dark glasses. Gold chain with a dollar sign. A golden staff with a blue stone at the tip. And, man, wearing sneakers.

Jack went still.

— Then he came at me with some riddle talk I didn't understand at all. Then he turned to the side and started arguing with someone who wasn't there. Lost the argument. And turned back with a completely different voice: I came to slay. I came to diva, flashy as always.

Jack opened his mouth. Closed it.

— Said he was Pajé Yawanari, of the Xakaxi tribe. That he was the ten out of ten who was in charge of protecting all the sacred powers of this whole world. — Kim said, serious. — And that the problem I was going through was just a flea on the monkey's ass.

Jack hit the ground.

Literally. Slipped off the curb and fell sitting on the asphalt, laughing the laugh that comes from a deep place and doesn't ask permission.

— I LOST IT. I'm in Disneyland. What a wild trip!

Kim held his serious face as long as he could. Until he remembered that beard, those glasses, that dollar sign on his chest. The laugh came out before he could do anything.

Even there, almost dying, he had already thought: if I get out of this alive, I'm going to laugh a lot at the way that old man talks.

---

Jack got back on the curb with what dignity he could. Wiped his eye.

And looked at his friend. Serious again.

— That old man talked about your family, didn't he?

— It's fucked up. He did. — Kim said, head down, hands together. — Said I was just like my grandfather. That, like my grandfather, I'm a little badass. Grandson of the big badass. — He looked at the street. — If he knows about my grandfather, he knows about my father. Made me want to ask Helena.

Jack scratched his head. He'd always known that was a sensitive subject.

— Man, I know you don't like talking about that. So I won't push anything, like I never have. I'll just tell you one thing. Whatever decision you make, talking to your mom or not, finding out who your dad is or not... you know I'm here, right?

Kim smiled. Once again, happy to have that friend.

— Yeah, man. Yeah. It's weird for me to talk about that guy. I don't even know who he is, or what happened between him and Helena. And that's it. Just that. That's why I don't like people asking me about something I don't even have a place to start answering.

---

— You're gonna try today. — Jack said, without looking at him.

— I am.

— Okay. Text me if you need anything.

— I won't need to.

— I know. Text anyway.

Jack got up from the curb, dusted off his pants and walked off down the dark street with the drumsticks in his back pocket.

— Show up alive tomorrow in time for class. — Kim said, loud.

Jack raised his hand without turning.

Kim gathered the cups, went home alone and closed the garage gate.

He stood for a second with his hand on the cold iron. Inside, in the dark, the guitar hanging on the nail, the bass leaning against the wall, the drums with the hole where a tom was missing.

For the first time, the garage seemed too small.
