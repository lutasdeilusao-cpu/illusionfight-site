# CHAPTER 17 — Poor Man's Cuba

Kim and Nina looked at Jack and went with it.

Jack led the way to a liquor store. The usual: a soda, some lemons, a bottle of vodka. They stopped at DIX. Then they went to their quiet spot, where nobody bothered them. No enemies, no people looking for a fight, no cops.

An empty parking lot. Some old abandoned cars: a Beetle with no wheels, a pickup with a tree growing out of the engine, a red hatchback with the back door permanently open. And what was left of a police station, the sign half-painted over. The cars were impounded from back when it was still operating.

It was Kim and Jack's refuge. And, since the night of the band, Nina's too.

Kim took the cups from his backpack. Jack cut the lemons with his pocketknife. Nina opened the soda.

Vodka first. Lemon squeezed by hand. Stir with a finger. Top it off with soda.

— Poor man's Cuba. — Jack raising his cup.

The three took their first sips almost at the same time. They adjusted the drink, more lemon, less lemon. Everyone knew without saying that this wasn't a day to drink until they dropped. They set their cups on the ground.

— So. — Jack. — We were talking about the insane weapons Kim managed to spawn mid-match. — He turned to Nina. — Look, Nina, I'm not defending him. But Kim doesn't know anything about this game. He has no way of knowing how to hack a weapon into the middle of a fight. It was spontaneous. How? Mystery. Like a lot of things in LDI.

He took a light sip. Turned to Kim.

— And you. I know you're still not used to it. But just accept that Nina is already practically part of our crew. Of the gang. Of our group. The sooner you accept it, the easier it'll be to cuss her out in the future without anyone taking it to heart. Same way I cuss you out and you cuss me out. All right?

Kim and Nina looked at each other.

Neither said anything. But they both drank at the same time, which was the same thing.

---

— Now the good part. — Jack. — Kim discovered Xamã mode on his own. The first time he picked up a weapon. And already used it like it was mid-level weapon technique. That's absurd for a beginner. I thought it would be a lot more work. If he only knew, for example, that I had to charge my attack...

— That was messed up, for real. — Kim. — Training environment. You were supposed to teach me everything first. And you two came at me throwing powers when I didn't know how to use anything. Every fighting game lets you learn at least one hadouken before it beats the crap out of you.

Nina and Jack laughed in his face.

— Okay. So let's start from the beginning. — Jack stood up, cup in hand, like a prep-course teacher. — Combat modes. There are three. First is free-hand mode. That's what everyone uses at the start. That's what you tried in the fight against Thunderbolt. Second is weapon mode, which you just used. And third is power mode, which is using your elemental to attack.

— Which I've never used.

— You did. In the first fight. With that crazy transformation. That didn't happen today. — Jack paused. — Right. Why didn't we talk about that first?

Kim looked at his cup.

— I don't remember what happened after I blacked out in that fight. And today I didn't even notice I was different. What was that? Is that part of the game?

— I don't know what that was. — Nina, lower. — I don't even know if it's allowed. But you're the only person I've ever seen do something outside the rules like that. I wouldn't doubt it's forbidden for everyone and only you can do it.

— The corporation says the game is mental strength. — Jack. — What I think is you had more mental strength than the game expected. And it interpreted that that way. It was so strong you don't even remember.

— That's what I don't get. — Kim. — How does a game measure my mental strength? What's the difference between mine and yours?

— For you, from the outside, it seems complicated. — Jack. — And you still don't have experience. But for me, and I think for Nina too, who's had a few fights, you can see how much our energy capacity changes. And energy is everything in LDI. Only, since we don't use our bodies in there, just our heads, your energy doesn't come from physical training. It comes from mental strength. Pure. How the corporation calculates that? I have no idea.

---

Jack stirred his drink with his finger and started walking in front of the two of them.

— Think with me. When you start a fight, you're at your best, right?

— Of course. That's why we warm up first.

— Exactly. Best shape. Then the fight goes on and you get tired. Your body spends itself. Losing strength.

— Obviously. Get to the point.

— Easy. I'll get there. — Jack raised his hand. — In LDI it's the same. You start at full energy and spend it as you go. Spend to hit, spend to use a weapon, spend to use power. Only there's a modifier. The stronger your mental strength, the more energy you have to spend. And there are rare cases, which I've seen, where a guy is at his limit, done, and out of nowhere accesses more power than he had at the start. I never fully understood it. But it's visible when you're in there.

Nina slowly lowered her cup. She had the still eyes of someone who just put two things together.

— It's true. — Over Jack. — I'd never thought about that. There are two types. There's the mental strength that gives a lot of power the whole time, from start to finish. And there's the explosive kind. The person starts out weaker and keeps releasing more than they had during the fight. It's a modifier. How did I never think of that?

Jack smiled, proud of the conclusion. Modesty was never his strong suit.

— How to train that? Don't ask me, my dear. I don't know that. — He stopped in front of Kim. — But I do know one thing. Since our first fight, I saw that you were very strong. Not because of your body. Because of your head. You're determined in a way I've never seen in anyone. And in LDI that becomes power. I always knew that the day you went in there, you were going to be something to fear.

He raised his cup. He felt a little awkward about what he'd just said.

Nina thought that was beautiful. She raised her cup too.

Kim raised his.

The three clinked.

---

— Now. Energy. — Jack, recovering. — To do anything beyond human limits, you spend energy. Speed, heavy hits, everything. Only there's no bar. No number on screen. You feel it in your body. The tiredness. The strength draining away. Same as real life. — He took a sip. — My mouth is dry. Nina, talk about free-hand.

— Free-hand is the normal. — Nina. — It's the weapon everyone already has. It's how you guys fight every day, right, boys? It uses less energy, because you're not using anything beyond what you already know, just amplified. But the more superhuman you want to be, the more it costs. There are players who spend more energy on a free-hand punch than others spend using a power. Because they're going way beyond what they'd normally do. — She took a sip. — Damn. Talking this much really dries your throat.

— So free-hand is the best? — Kim. — I'm good with my hands and spend less.

Jack and Nina looked at each other and laughed. They liked that innocence of his.

— I wish, man. — Jack. — If it were just that, the best fighters in the world would be the LDI champions. They're not. I beat you today without throwing a single punch. And with Nina you suffered. Easy. Don't start getting cocky.

Kim thought about it. He was right.

— But for you, who already knows how to fight, it makes sense to start free-hand. To read the opponent and decide how much you want to spend in that fight. — Jack. — Just watch out for the explosive type. If a guy is hiding his power and lets it all out at once, you're not prepared and you're screwed. And in free-hand, if you really commit, you can get more speed, more strength, and catch your opponent by surprise. Did I miss anything, Nina?

— No, my dear. That's it, gorgeous.

— Ooh. She called me gorgeous. — Jack with his hand on his chest. — So let's move on to weapon mode. Nina is the perfect person for this. Take it away, Nina.

---

Nina made a face like she was getting bored.

— Guys, all this is in the manual. He opens SBI and reads. Why do we have to explain everything to him?

— Think with me, Nina. — Jack very serious. — Imagine that in an alternate universe we don't exist. And our story is being told in a book. If we don't explain to Kim how it works, whoever's reading won't understand anything. So that's why we have to explain everything properly.

Kim and Nina looked at Jack like he'd gone crazy.

The drink was getting to him.

Jack motioned for her to continue. Nina continued, because everyone knows you don't argue with crazy people.

— It starts with buying the weapon. Everyone starts with a starter weapon. A little knife, the same for everyone. It's garbage. You can call it any time, but after a few moves it eats up all your energy and does nothing. So you fight. In LDI, even the loser earns points. About five percent of what the winner got. You can use those points to climb in SDR or to buy items.

— And you buy the weapon.

— You buy a pack. You don't choose the weapon. You buy a closed pack with five different weapons, and you pick one blind. One in five chance of getting the one you wanted. And the packs are expensive, just a heads-up. Each weapon comes with a card that explains how it works in normal mode and in Xamã mode. Then you use it in an official fight. — She looked at him. — And you can't buy or draw a weapon mid-match. Not even in training. That's why we're impressed. You didn't buy anything. And the weapon appeared.

Jack, seeing that subject was still sore, stepped in.

— And then you name it. I have my Vitória. Nina has Angel. You have Xakixi. Nice name, by the way. When you set the name, the weapon bonds to you. LDI recognizes it. Whenever you think the name or say the name, it appears. And when you call it, it stays with you the whole match. The first transformation into Xamã has to be spoken aloud, so it learns. After that, just thinking is enough.

— It reads my thoughts.

— Kim. The whole game reads your thoughts.

Kim sat with that for a second. A weapon that read minds. A game that read minds. It made as much sense as everything else.

— And the weapon shares your energy. — Jack continued. — The more it needs to attack or block, the more it takes from you. And you already spend energy on quick movements; with a weapon that draws more, the cost goes up. Xamã takes even more. If you stay in Xamã the whole time, you dry up fast. That's why it goes back to normal when you're not using it. Got it? Xamã mode is like Super Saiyan. The weapon's max. Clear?

Kim nodded.

— In our fight I used Angel in Xamã. — Nina. — In Xamã she stretches like an arm, up to about three meters. And then I combined it with my elemental, which is Light, and made Luminus Blade. The weapon with the power combined. To make sure I'd cut you in half.

— That I remember. — Kim. — It was way too close. Thanks for reminding me. — Pause. — And that's when I saw the wise old man again. The one who speaks in riddles.

Jack stopped his cup halfway.

— So, old man? What was the deal this time?

— Oh, right. I haven't told you yet. I had it on my mind, but you guys started talking about power, about modes, and I forgot. In the middle of our fight, when Nina's light was on me, I spoke again with that crazy Indian.

Nina put her hand on his shoulder.

— Kim. Don't take this the wrong way. But you know you have strong indigenous ancestry, right?

Kim looked at her like he wasn't getting the question.

— Of course I know. I'm not calling him crazy because he's Indian. I'm calling him crazy because nobody talks the way this guy talks. Nobody on the face of the Earth. He's like me, only crazy. I'm not.

— Okay. — Nina removing her hand, relieved. — I just said it so nobody thinks you were bad-mouthing someone because of their origin.

*Jack's craziness catches on.*

— Don't worry. — Kim. — Assholes aren't assholes because of skin color, race, origin, gender, money. They're assholes because they're assholes. Because they like it. They're assholes deep down and they invent a reason to pretend they're right. Some realize it and try to change. Some die assholes. And some know they are and hide among everyone, nameless, to keep being that way.

Jack nodded slowly.

— Okay, guys. You're getting off topic. Let's keep going, I want to finish soon. I want to go home, take a shower, rest a bit. And you and I still have to work tonight, Kim. Probably until about two in the morning at the club.

Kim and Nina shut their mouths.

— So, Kim. You can combine styles. Nina combined weapon with power. You can combine free-hand with power. Or free-hand with weapon. And other combinations you might think of that I don't. The important thing with a weapon is using it well. Most of the time, at the right moment. Today me and Nina overused it to show you. But in an official fight, I call Vitória at a specific moment. — He drank. — I always fight with maximum energy economy in mind. I'm very strong, but I don't have that much mental energy to spend. I've already noticed that. So I balance and use my power at that sweet spot. Maybe that's something you'll have to figure out in yourself. Every avatar is different, just like every person is different. In LDI you have to find the balance point of your character. Cool? Got it? Let's move on to power mode?

Kim was going to ask him to continue.

But Nina was already straightening up, her eyes lighting up for the first time since they'd arrived.

— This one's mine. — Nina. — This is my favorite.

Her eyes had changed. It was the first time that day that Nina looked her age.

— If you're like me, who grew up watching cartoons, anime, movies about people with superpowers... This is my theory for why LDI is what it is. It gave superpowers to anyone. Anywhere in the world. Anyone.

Kim was fully paying attention.

— When you create the avatar, the system classifies you into an elemental. Primary or secondary. And there's no difference in strength between them, because, like Jack said, everything comes from your energy. The power's strength is how much energy it takes from you and puts into it. Same as a weapon. You gather energy into the power and release it.

— And there are types.

— Three. — She counted on her fingers. — Instant. Medium duration. Long duration. The longer it takes to charge, the more damage it usually does. But the longer you're open in the middle of a fight. And depending on the power, if you take a hit during the charge, you lose everything. The power and the energy you'd already put into it. That's why it's very risky to use long-duration powers in a real fight.
