# CHAPTER 17 — Poor Man's Cuba Libre

Kim and Nina looked at Jack and went along.

Jack led the way to a liquor store. The usual: a soda, some lemons, a bottle of vodka. They stopped by DIX. Then they went to their quiet spot, where nobody bothered them. No enemies, no people looking for a fight, no cops.

An empty parking lot. Old abandoned cars, a Beetle with no wheels, a pickup with a tree growing out of the engine, a red hatchback with the rear door open forever. And what was left of a police station, the sign half painted over. The cars were impounded from back when it was open.

It was Kim and Jack's refuge. Nina's first time there.

Kim took the cups from his backpack. Jack sliced the lemons with his pocketknife. Nina opened the soda.

Vodka first. Lemon squeezed by hand. Stir with your finger. Top it off with soda.

— Poor man's Cuba Libre. — Jack raising his glass.

The three took their first sip almost at the same time. They adjusted the drink, more lemon, less lemon. Everyone knew, without anyone saying it, that today was not a day to drink until they dropped. They set the cups on the ground.

— So. — Jack. — We were talking about the sick weapons Kim managed to spawn mid-match. — He turned to Nina. — Look, Nina, I'm not defending him. But Kim doesn't know anything about this game. He has no way of knowing how to hack to pull a weapon out mid-fight. It was spontaneous. How? Mystery. Like a lot of things in LDI.

He took a small sip. Turned to Kim.

— And you. I know you're not used to it yet. But just accept that Nina is basically part of our crew. The gang. Our group. The sooner you accept it, the easier it'll be to call her names in the future without anyone taking it to heart. Same way I talk shit to you and you talk shit to me. Deal?

Kim and Nina looked at each other.

Neither said anything. But they both drank at the same time, which was the same thing.

---

— Now the good part. — Jack. — Kim discovered Shaman mode on his own. The first time he picked up a weapon. And he already used it like a mid-level weapon. That's insane for a beginner. I thought I'd have a lot more work. If he knew, for example, that I had to charge my attack...

— That was bullshit, honestly. — Kim. — Training environment. You were supposed to teach me everything first. And you came at me throwing powers while I didn't know how to use anything. Every fighting game lets you learn at least one hadouken before it beats you down.

Nina and Jack laughed in his face.

— Okay. So let's start from the beginning. — Jack stood up, cup in hand, like a prep-course teacher. — Combat modes. There are three. The first is free-hand. That's what everyone uses at the start. That's what you tried in the fight against Thunderbolt. The second is weapon mode, which you just used. And the third is power mode, which is using your elemental to attack.

— Which I've never used.

— You did. In your first fight. With that crazy transformation. Which didn't happen today. — Jack stopped. — That's true. Why didn't we talk about that first?

Kim looked at his cup.

— I don't remember what happened after I blacked out in that fight. And today I didn't even notice I was different. What was that? Is that part of the game?

— I don't know what it was. — Nina, lower. — I don't even know if it's allowed. But you're the only person I've ever seen do something outside the rules like that. I wouldn't doubt that it's forbidden for everyone and only you can do it.

— The corporation says the game is mental strength. — Jack. — What I think is you had more mental strength than the game expected. And it interpreted that like that. It was so strong that even you don't remember.

— That's what I don't get. — Kim. — How does a game measure my mental strength? What's the difference between mine and yours?

— To you, on the outside, it seems complicated. — Jack. — And you still don't have experience. But to me, and I think to Nina too, who's had a few fights, you can see how much our energy capacity changes. And energy is everything in LDI. Except, since we don't use our bodies in there, only our heads, your energy doesn't come from physical training. It comes from mental strength. Pure. How the corporation calculates that? I have no idea.

---

Jack stirred his drink with his finger and started walking in front of the other two.

— Think with me. When you start a fight, you're at your best, right?

— Of course. That's why we warm up first.

— Right. Best shape. Then the fight goes on and you get tired. Your body gets worn down. Losing strength.

— Obviously. Get to the point.

— Easy. I'll get there. — Jack raised his hand. — In LDI it's the same. You start at full energy and it gets spent. You spend it to hit, to use weapons, to use powers. But there's a modifier. The stronger your mental strength, the more energy you have to spend. And there are rare cases, which I've seen, where a guy is at his limit, finished, and out of nowhere accesses more power than he had at the start. I never really understood it. But it's visible when you're in there.

Nina slowly lowered her cup. Her eyes were still, like someone who'd just put two things together.

— It's true. — Over Jack. — I'd never thought of that. There are two types. There's the mental strength that gives a lot of power the whole time, from start to finish. And there's the explosive kind. The person starts weaker and releases more than they had during the fight. It's a modifier. How did I never think of that?

Jack smiled, proud of the conclusion. Modesty was never his strong suit.

— How do you train that? Don't ask me, my dear. I don't know. — He stopped in front of Kim. — But I know one thing. Since our first fight, I saw that you were very strong. Not because of your body. Because of your head. You're determined in a way I've never seen in anyone. And in LDI that turns into power. I always knew that the day you entered that place, you'd be something to fear.

He raised his cup. He got a little embarrassed about what he'd just said.

Nina thought it was sweet. She raised her cup too.

Kim raised his.

The three toasted.

---

— Now. Energy. — Jack, recovering. — To do something beyond human limits, you spend energy. Speed, heavy hits, everything. But there's no bar. No number on the screen. You feel it in your body. The fatigue. The strength draining away. Just like real life. — He took a sip. — My mouth's dry. Nina, talk about free-hand.

— Free-hand is the normal one. — Nina. — It's the weapon everyone already has. It's how you guys fight every day, right, boys? It spends less energy, because you're not using anything beyond what you already know, just amplified. But the more superhuman you want to be, the more it costs. There are players who spend more energy on a free-hand punch than others spend using a power. Because they're going way beyond what they'd normally do. — She took a sip. — Man. Talking this much really dries your throat.

— So free-hand is the best? — Kim. — I'm good with my hands and I spend less.

Jack and Nina looked at each other and laughed. They liked that innocence of his.

— If only, man. — Jack. — If it were just that, the best fighters in the world would be the LDI champions. They're not. I beat you today without throwing a punch. And with Nina you struggled. Relax. Don't start getting cocky.

Kim thought. He had a point.

— But for you, who already knows how to fight, it makes sense to start free-hand. To read your opponent and decide how much you want to spend in that fight. — Jack. — But watch out for the explosive type. If a guy's hiding his power and unleashes it all at once, you're not ready and you're fucked. And in free-hand, if you commit, you can get more speed, more strength, and catch your opponent by surprise. Did I miss anything, Nina?

— No, my dear. That's it, handsome.

— Ooh. She called me handsome. — Jack with his hand on his chest. — So let's move on to weapon mode. Nina's the perfect person for this. Go ahead, Nina.

---

Nina made the face of someone getting bored.

— Guys, all this is in the manual. He can open the SBI and read it. Why do we have to explain everything to him?

— Think with me, Nina. — Jack very serious. — Imagine that in an alternate universe we don't exist. And our story is being told in a book. If we don't explain to Kim how it works, whoever's reading won't understand anything. So that's why we have to explain everything nice and clear.

Kim and Nina looked at Jack like he'd gone crazy.

The drink was getting to him.

Jack motioned for her to continue. Nina continued, because everyone knows you don't argue with crazy people.

— You start by buying a weapon. Everyone starts with a starter weapon. A little knife, the same for everyone. It's garbage. You can call it whenever, but after a few moves it eats all your energy and does nothing. Then you fight. In LDI, even the loser earns points. About five percent of what the winner earned. You can use those points to rank up in the SDR or to buy items.

— And you buy the weapon.

— You buy a pack. You don't choose the weapon. You buy a pack with five different weapons, sealed, and you pick one blind. One in five chance of getting the one you wanted. And the packs are expensive, just warning you. Each weapon comes with a card that explains how it works in normal mode and in Shaman mode. Then you use it in an official fight. — She looked at him. — And you can't buy or pull out a weapon mid-match. Not even in training. That's why we're impressed. You didn't buy anything. And the weapon appeared.

Jack, seeing that subject still stung, jumped in.

— And then you name it. I have my Vitória. Nina has Angel. You have Xakixi. Pretty name, by the way. When you set the name, the weapon bonds to you. LDI recognizes it. Whenever you think the name or say the name, it appears. And when you call it, it stays with you the whole match. The first Shaman transformation has to be spoken, out loud, for it to learn. After that, you just think it.

— It reads my thoughts.

— Kim. The whole game reads your thoughts.

Kim sat with that for a second. A weapon that read minds. A game that read minds. It made as much sense as everything else.

— And the weapon splits your energy. — Jack continued. — The more it needs to attack or block, the more it drains from you. And you already spend energy on fast movements; with a weapon that pulls more, the cost goes up. Shaman drains even more. If you stay in Shaman the whole time, you dry out fast. That's why it goes back to normal when you're not using it. Got it? Shaman mode is like Super Saiyan. The weapon's max. Clear?

Kim nodded.

— In our fight I used Angel in Shaman mode. — Nina. — In Shaman it stretches like an arm, up to about three meters. And then I combined it with my elemental, which is Light, and made the Luminus Blade. The weapon with the power together. To make sure I'd cut you in half.

— That I remember. — Kim. — It was close. Thanks for reminding me. — Pause. — And that's when I saw the old sage again. The one who speaks in riddles.

Jack stopped his cup halfway.

— So, old man? What was the deal this time?

— Oh, right. I haven't told you yet. I had it on my mind, but you started talking about power, modes, and I forgot. In the middle of our fight, when Nina's light was on me, I talked again to that crazy Native guy.

Nina put her hand on his shoulder.

— Kim. Don't take this the wrong way. But you know you have strong indigenous ancestry, right?

Kim looked at her like he didn't understand the question.

— Of course I know. I'm not calling him crazy because he's Native. I'm calling him crazy because nobody talks the way this guy talks. Nobody on the face of the Earth. He's just like me, only crazy. I'm not.

— Okay. — Nina pulled her hand back, relieved. — I just said it so nobody would think you were badmouthing someone because of his background.

*Jack's craziness is contagious.*

— Don't sweat it. — Kim. — Assholes aren't assholes because of color, race, background, gender, money. They're assholes because they're assholes. Because they like it. They're assholes deep down and invent a reason to pretend they're right. Some realize it and try to change. Some die assholes. And some know they are and hide in the middle of everyone, nameless, so they can keep being.

Jack nodded slowly.

— Okay, guys. You're getting off topic. Let's keep going, I want to finish soon. I want to go home, take a shower, rest a little. And you and I still have work tonight, Kim. Probably until about two in the morning at the club.

Kim and Nina shut up.

— So, Kim. You can combine styles. Nina combined weapon with power. You can combine free-hand with power. Or free-hand with weapon. And other combinations you might think of that I don't. The important thing with a weapon is using it well. Most of the time, at the right moment. Today Nina and I overdid it to show you. But in an official fight, I summon Vitória at a specific moment. — He drank. — I always fight with maximum energy economy. I'm very strong, but I don't have that much mental energy to spend. I've noticed that. So I balance and use power at just the right point. Maybe that's something you'll have to figure out in yourself. Every avatar is different, just like every person is different. In LDI you have to find your character's balance point. Cool? Make sense? Let's move on to power mode?

Kim was about to ask him to keep going.

But Nina was already straightening up, her eyes lighting up for the first time since they'd arrived.

— That one's mine. — Nina. — That's my favorite.

Her eyes had changed. It was the first time that day that Nina seemed her age.

— If you're like me, who grew up watching cartoons, anime, movies about people with superpowers... That's my theory for why LDI is what it is. It gave superpowers to anyone. Anywhere in the world. Anyone.

Kim was paying full attention.

— When you create your avatar, the system classifies you into an elemental. Primary or secondary. And there's no power difference between them, because, like Jack said, everything comes from your energy. The strength of a power is how much energy it drains from you and puts into it. Same as a weapon. You gather energy into the power and release it.

— And there are types.

— Three. — She counted on her fingers. — Instant. Medium duration. Long duration. The longer it takes to charge, the more damage it usually does. But the longer you're open in the middle of a fight. And depending on the power, if you take a hit mid-charge, you lose everything. The power and the energy you'd already put into it. That's why it's very risky to use long-duration powers in a real fight.
