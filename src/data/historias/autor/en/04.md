# CHAPTER 4 — The Last Step

> ⚠️ **SENSITIVE CONTENT.** This chapter deals with bullying, physical violence in childhood and a knife inside a school. If this subject hurts you, you can stop here.

---

I'd just finished preschool.

My parents managed to pay for it. It was cheap, and a lot of kids from church went. My parents were church people. They still are.

I was always fast. I started reading really early, around 5, before first grade. Real reading.

Then I went to first grade, at a public school next to the favela, the Jorge Andrade.

It went wrong. From day one.

---

I was a huge nerd. A total bookworm.

And the bullying started. A lot of it.

The kids there had more street smarts. More cruelty. More life. Same age, different experience.

I suffered like hell, because I had no idea life was like that.

---

Then came my first beating.

I'd taken a few slaps before. But this one stuck. This one changed my life.

After it, I started getting beaten. A lot.

Until fourth grade I was the doormat. The full bullied bookworm. The kind you see in Korean shows today.

Except back then nobody said bullying was that heavy. It was just how it was. Life is crazy: you're weak, you suffer.

And later I found out it was never about strength.

---

In fourth grade I took my last beating.

I remember it clearly.

We went up a staircase, the kids mocking me, pushing me along. At the top, on the last step, one of them punched me right in the face.

And I went down tumbling.

They wanted to see it. Me getting hurt. Breaking all over.

And something broke inside me too.

Broke deep.

While I rolled down those stairs, I told myself:

This won't happen anymore. Not to me, never again. I'm smart enough to change this. And I will.

Fourth grade.

---

It caused a huge uproar. The kids got suspended for a long time. I barely saw them until the end of the year.

And a lot of kids there were already hustling. Today people talk about minors working, but even back then kids started hustling really early.

Back then, that school went from first to fourth grade. The next year, everyone moved. I went to another school, the Carolina.

And I'd already figured it out: every public school in the area is the same hustle.

So I spent the whole break training. Intense physical training.

I read a lot, remember? One of the books I read was a Bruce Lee training book. Long before Ip Man and all that hype. I read a lot about physical training.

I was never jacked. Never tall. I'm not that short either, okay?

And I only ever fought tall guys. Tall guys think they're something. It pisses me off. Today I have plenty of tall friends, no prejudice. But I had a lot of trouble with tall guys.

---

Some kids from the crew that bullied me went to the Carolina too.

And they thought the game would continue in fifth grade.

We weren't in the same class. But at recess they came at me. I kept walking away, ignoring them, making it clear: don't mess with me.

And they started marking me. Day after day.

In their heads it was simple: "that's our doormat, are we letting him get away? No."

They weren't about to lose their doormat.

The old leader had gone to another school. Now there was a new kid wanting to take over the crew.

By the third week of school I knew it was growing. That they wanted to put all of that back into my life.

---

> ⚠️ **SENSITIVE CONTENT FROM HERE ON.** A knife and a threat inside a school.

So I took a knife to school.

Yeah.

---

I was on my way to the library to get a book.

Damn, I was such a nerd. It pisses me off just remembering. Not that I don't like myself back then. But I was really soft.

They saw me.

"Hey, Isaias, what's up? Come here."

I looked at them very calmly.

And it was the first time the world went gray.

I thought: okay. Take the wheel. You've been preparing for this. Let's see you in action.

I don't control him. He shows up when the situation shows up. But that first time I didn't really understand it. I was even polite about it. I let him take over.

In those moments I'm there. It's not that it isn't me. It's me, damn it. But I'm in the passenger seat. I don't see everything. I don't remember everything in order. A lot of it I rebuild later, from what people tell me.

---

I grabbed the kid by his shirt.

A shirt that's firm enough: you twist it, turn your wrist, it catches and locks in just right. And starts choking. The guy's first reaction is to grab his own neck. And then his face is wide open.

The school looked like a prison, full of bars. I pinned him against one.

And I put the knife to his neck.

"What the fuck? What are you doing?"

"I told you not to mess with me. After what you did to me, you know what you deserve. But I won't do it. Not today. Today it's just a warning. Because if I start, I won't stop. I'll break the record for stab wounds on a human being."

Then I looked at the other kids.

It's always like that: there's a little punk leader and some kids who follow him. You take the leader, and the rest start thinking about their own safety.

And I was making it clear I didn't care about mine. If I had to, I'd run over everything. He just had to give me a reason.

"Listen up. You and everyone here. If any of you messes with me again, next time you won't even see it coming. I won't show up when the fight starts. I'll get you one by one. On your way home. When you go get a drink of water. I'll take you down calmly, with pleasure and with precision."

I let him go.

He didn't know what to say.

I turned around, tucked the knife in my waistband and went to the library to get my book.

Those kids never messed with me again.

Never.

---

That's when I learned something about bullying.

I'd already reached that conclusion in theory. It was the first time I left theory, went into practice, and the result was good.

Anyone can be a victim. But they want the easy ones.

If you're a victim who's a lot of work, they leave you alone.

Because bullies are weak. Lazy. They don't have the resilience it takes.

That day I understood that.

---

"But you took a knife to school. You're dangerous."

No.

To me, a knife is a defense tool for whoever's weaker. It evens things out. The guys are stronger, I need to even it out.

That's my street fight rule. The first thing I look at: is the guy big? Is there a rock? A stick? A pipe? A chain? Whatever's there, I'm using it.

You have no idea what I'm like in a fight.

Street fights have no rules.

Am I going to give the guy an advantage? Let him mount me just to be honorable? Fuck honor. I want to land the hits. I want to see the blood running.

And if I'm going to take a beating, I'll take it and enjoy it. Hit, fall, get up. Fall, get up. Until the guy gets tired.

That's how I like my fights.

---

That crew never messed with me again. But other things happened. I discovered another side of myself at the Carolina and explored it a lot.

I raised so much hell I got expelled. The first school I ever got expelled from.

Except expulsion from a public school doesn't work the way you'd imagine. So you don't officially go down as expelled, they "invite you to leave." From what I saw later, working in a school, it's still like that today.

The principal herself found me a new school and said:

"You're not doing sixth grade here. You'll do it at another school. I don't want to see you here anymore."

It was formalized with a teachers' council and everything. Expulsion.

But my record says "transfer." They don't even write "invited to leave." Too bad. I have no official record of it.

I went to the Miguel, and that's where another phase of my life begins. Another story.

And you know the funniest part?

Years later, I became a hall monitor at the Carolina. A civil servant, through a public exam. I stayed there almost 12 years.

The same school that expelled me.

Life is a slot car track.

---

**In the fiction, it became**

The first time the world went gray was in that hallway, in fifth grade. That's where Kim's gray world comes from.

And the rule of the rock, the stick, the pipe and the chain is the same as in [*The Only Rule*](/historias/lutas-de-ilusao/capitulo-03): street fights have no rules.
