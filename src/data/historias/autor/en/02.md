# CHAPTER 2 — The World Went Gray

This is one of the most dramatic stories I can tell.

And one of the funniest.

I know it'll seem weird when you read it. But to me this is a happy story.

---

It's the crowd fight I took on alone.

I got beaten like a dog.

I've never taken a beating like that in my life. That's how I knew that day was the peak of my survival.

---

I was 18 and had my first official girlfriend.

Before her I'd had some hookups, some little flings. Nothing serious. I was a wild kid.

One day she got invited to a party by a guy from her job. Far as hell. Rural area, middle of the woods.

She said: "I'm not going without you." Let's go. A party is us.

A party? Let's go. I drank, I smoked, I loved that stuff. Back then, even more.

We took a bunch of buses. Dirt road, nothing but farms around. A hard place to get to.

---

The guy had set up something really nice.

Big house, huge property and a giant garage. The whole party was in the garage. His parents' house stayed closed.

Lots of booze, lots of food, over a hundred people. We didn't bring anything, as usual. All on the house.

I was chilling. Drinking, eating, dancing with my girlfriend. I really like dancing.

---

At some point she said:

"I need to pee, I'm going to the bathroom."

The bathroom was inside the house. I went with her.

There was a big guy at the door.

"She can use the bathroom. But only she goes in."

Fine. I was always short and skinny. I waited.

She took a while.

And came back weird.

The vibe wasn't the same as before the bathroom.

"Let's go."

There was still gas left to burn at that party. Food, drinks. I'm kind of a starving type, you know? If there's food, I'm in.

"You're acting weird. What happened?"

"I'm not telling you. You'll start trouble."

"I won't. I'm chill, I'm eating, I'm dancing. Tell me."

She told me.

As she was coming out of the bathroom, the host cornered her inside the house. And tried to force a kiss on her.

---

Now I need to explain something about me.

There are several Isaiases inside me. And there's one who only shows up when the world goes gray.

I let that guy out. He takes the wheel.

He doesn't measure consequences. He has no limits. He'll solve it the way it needs to be solved.

When she finished talking, the world went gray.

---

"Are you okay?"

"I'm fine."

I never let it show.

The only rule in a street fight is that there are no rules. So I don't give any advantage to the guy I'm about to beat up. No warning. No wasted words.

If I'm going to hit you, I'm just going to hit you.

The party stopped existing. My girlfriend too.

"I'm going to grab a little something, I'll be right back."

"Please don't start trouble."

---

From here on there are a lot of blackouts. I don't remember everything in order. I remember that it happened.

I didn't even know who the host was. We were only there because my girlfriend got invited.

I kept asking. He was at the door of the house.

"You're the host?"

No anger. Nothing showing.

"Yeah. Why? Did something happen?"

I just closed my fist.

I grabbed his shirt, because these guys keep running and chasing them is a pain. Big guy, gym-built, way taller than me.

And I started punching.

Feeling my fist sink in. Blood running down. Him hitting me and me not even caring.

Come on. Endurance test. Let's see who lasts.

---

Except it was his party.

His friends saw their friend getting beaten without understanding a thing. I don't give explanations.

And then I started getting hit from every direction.

Eyebrow splitting. Nose breaking. Mouth bursting. That metallic taste of blood.

Tasty, you know?

There are rules to survive a crowd fight. Instincts you have to follow. I'd fought in groups before, school against school, crew against crew. I knew the rules.

But I was in the gray world.

There was no analytical Isaias there to think about anything. I broke every rule.

Every time I got hit, I held on tighter. If he got away into the crowd, I'd never catch him again.

So I dragged him with me.

Punching. Turning him toward the crowd, so they'd hit him too, so they'd stop hitting me for a second. Spinning. Punching. Across the garage, the tables, the whole party.

Next thing I knew, we were in the middle of the dirt road.

---

Something I learned in practice: gym muscles don't mean strength.

They look nice. But strength, endurance, resilience? No.

Especially when the guy doesn't know how to throw a punch. I've trained a lot of martial arts in my life. The last one, and the one I took most seriously, was boxing. Knowing how to throw a punch changes everything in a fight.

But I was taking way too much.

I felt my leg twist. I remember thinking "fuck."

The screaming. The commotion. Me bleeding, shirt torn, covered in blood. Punching. Punching. Punching.

And not letting go.

---

The strength drained out little by little.

There comes a point where there's so much fighting that the crowd starts hitting each other. The fight becomes a virus. It spreads everywhere.

My body started giving out.

And the hand holding that son of a bitch went weak.

I let go.

I watched his body collapse.

And I thought: man, my job here is done.

---

I fell on my back. Let my body drop.

Fetal position. And I took every hit there was to take. The crowd wasn't satisfied.

I thought: these guys are going to beat me to death. And there's nothing I can do.

But at least he went down.

That's fair. We're even.

At some point they stopped. I wasn't dead yet.

---

I stayed on the ground for about three minutes.

Everyone staring. Starting to worry they'd actually killed me.

I used the time to breathe.

When I felt a little more rested, I got up out of nowhere. Covered in blood, all torn up.

And I started walking through them.

And they opened the way.

They really did. Because they understood what had happened. And nobody knew what was going to happen now.

Think about it: you see a guy who took a beating like that, who was lying on the ground, suddenly get up and walk off. You get wary. I remember the faces. Scared faces. "Damn, I don't know how to react" faces.

I'd already taken the bastard down. There was nothing left for me to do there.

I went to the table in the garage. Grabbed another liter bottle of beer.

And told my girlfriend:

"Let's go."

Spitting blood like crazy.

She was looking at me, scared. Not understanding anything.

---

I don't really remember the ride back. I kept blacking out and waking up on the bus the whole way. I didn't say a word to her.

We got to her place. I got in the shower. Blood already dried, mixed with mud.

I sat down. I was really tired, but numb from all the booze.

She was looking at me. Serious. Still cold.

And I was looking at her.

And I started laughing.

She went straight into the relationship talk:

"Seriously?"

"Seriously. The only thing we can do about this now is laugh."

"You're fucking crazy."

And she started laughing too.

Later I went to the hospital to get a few pieces stitched up. But I'm fine. It was fine.

---

This isn't an anime episode. It's not Wind Breaker.

It's real life. It happened.

I always thought about what I'd do if I ever had to face a whole group alone. I have rules for that.

That day I broke all of them.

Because I wasn't letting go of that guy. And I didn't. Not until he went limp on the ground, all messed up. Not until my strength ran out too.

I always go to the end.

The problem is they always break up my fights. Someone always shows up: "enough, enough." So I never know how far I go. I've never had the chance to go all the way.

A lot of times I took a beating only until the other guy got tired. He'd stop and say:

"Enough, aren't you going to stop?"

"Fuck off. Let's fight, damn it."

That day I did.

And after that I know: I'll survive anything in my life. Anything at all.

---
