# CHAPTER 1 — Where I Am Today

Fights of reality. Let's go.

This is hard to talk about.

---

This project has been a dream since I was 17. Watching it come out is fucking beautiful.

Even with my situation the way it is. Complicated. Very.

And I decided to tell it. Not so anyone feels sorry for me. I'm living this and I don't feel sorry for myself. I'm living this and I keep working every single day.

So keep your pity.

---

The games industry collapsed.

Mass layoffs at Epic Games. Then at Xbox, at Microsoft.

I felt it coming. The market was heading somewhere that didn't look stable. And video games were never stable: it all runs on projects. Keeping a product alive long term is one thing. Keeping it alive until one launch is another. It ships, the project ends, the job ends.

So I turned around. I moved into traditional software development.

Today I'm at [Panorama.id](https://panoramaid.com.br). A really cool company that gave me my career back. Outside of games. I don't know if I'll still be there by launch. But today I'm there.

And I love games. I love them so much this project has games in it. After working on God of War: Sons of Sparta and Five Nights at Freddy's: Into the Pit, it was obvious I'd go make the games I always dreamed of.

Except reality is harder than it looks.

---

Reality isn't pretty like the lives of Kim, Jack and Nina.

That's why I don't make their lives pretty. It would be forcing it.

I'm not knocking anyone who writes beautiful, happy stories. I admire it. I like reading them. But I can't spit that out from inside me.

---

Starting over has a price.

My salary dropped a lot. My cost of living is higher than it now.

So every day, at 6 p.m., I head out on my bike.

The Warrior. That's her name.

She works with me. She helps me a lot.

This is life, my people. I went back to the streets. And there's nothing wrong with it. Sometimes I even enjoy it. Not every day. But sometimes.

---

I started on the streets at 13.

I went back to the streets at 40.

In sixth, seventh grade, a friend of mine named Lester told me:

"Man, life is a slot car track, bro. You just go around."

About twenty-five years later, the lap came around.

---

But this isn't a sob story.

I'm not even counting on this product being a success. Honestly, success could even hurt: demand could show up that I can't handle the way it deserves, because I don't have the resources.

The story was born when I was 17. Three years ago I put out a book of it and didn't like it. I pulled it. I said: I'm doing it again, and this time differently.

It was supposed to be just a blog. I looked at where I could publish it as a web novel and thought: I'm not getting any visibility there. If there was no visibility, at least it would stay under my control.

Since June I've been coding this whole project.

Because what 17-year-old Isaias dreamed up was left for 40-year-old Isaias to deliver.

---

I'm not strong. I don't have superpowers. I don't do magic.

I go through hard times like everybody. And right now I'm going through some really extreme ones.

I always thought the peak of hardship in my life was a crowd fight I took on alone, around 19, 20. If I survived that, I'll survive anything.

I'll tell you that one in the next chapter.

---

My stories won't be in order. It'll be whatever I feel like telling. Whatever I think is wise to tell. Because there's a lot of heavy stuff.

And I'll be honest, like I always am. I won't fictionalize. I won't lie. Only facts.

Some of them you'll read and think: no way. That's normal. It didn't happen to you.

Jumping off a bridge. Taking on a big religious ministry. And, who knows, almost paying a little visit to the system.

We make choices. I don't regret mine, and I live with the consequences.

Resilient to the end.

---

You can see it.

I work at Panorama.id. Then I work on iFood until midnight, every single day. Sometimes into the early morning.

And every second I have left, I work on the project from my phone, with artificial intelligence, in remote-control mode. The phone does an iFood run and, at the same time, works on my project. I build, test, make a delivery, test again, make another one.

That's how everything you're getting comes out. WEB SHARD, the music, the stories. LDI Gangues, Top Trumps. The games of my life, the ones I always wanted to make and never had the chance.

All with my art direction. Direction is what I can do, because I can't draw. At the end of the first WEB SHARD chapter there are about 15 pages, fanzine style, where I explain why I use AI for this creation.

I stopped making excuses to myself. And I started doing it.

---

This isn't here to show suffering.

It's here to make a point.

You'll always have great excuses not to do something. The best in the world. All valid.

But when you want something done, there's only one way.

Do it.

So, through the hardship, through the rain, like today, we keep going. I keep making things.

This first year is guaranteed for you, and a big part of it is already done. The first season will be complete, perfect and incredible. I guarantee it.

I really hope you like it.

If you don't, fuck it. It's my version. My view of the world. Everything I wanted to make.

I do everything. I love it. And I'm going to keep doing it.

Fuck your criticism.

---

**In the fiction, it became**

Jack's bike, the Warrior, that carries him through his hustles in [*Because Jack Chose So*](/historias/contos/05), is my Warrior.

And Lester's slot car track keeps going around.
