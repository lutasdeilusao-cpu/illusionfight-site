# CHAPTER 1 — THE MARK

### NARRATOR: FLAW

From the first time I saw Minus, he saw me back.

Throughout the ages, I witnessed others being touched. I saw that mark form—the spiral burning into the skin of impure ones who would never understand what they carried. I observed some over centuries, wandering through their world.

But none ever looked back.

Until Minus.

When that thing—that nameless force even I cannot comprehend—touched him, something different happened. For a fraction of a second, so brief it could have been illusion, he didn't just receive the mark.

He looked back.

Saw something beyond. Something greater. Something that made his baby eyes widen in pure terror before returning to normal, before he began crying like any newborn.

But I felt it. Felt the moment when that thing realized it had been seen back.

And that had never happened before.

My name... Hmm... My name is Flaw. I gave myself that name, because that's what I am — a flaw in the order of things, a penumbra drifting between worlds. Before finding Minus, I simply drifted through the world of the impure, observing, without purpose, without direction.

But then I saw him. And he saw me back.

Perhaps—just perhaps—what he glimpsed in that infinitesimal second is the answer to what I am. To why I exist.

And since that day, I follow him.

### NARRATOR: MINUS · MY 4 YEARS OLD

Mom smelled like vanilla.

I don't know why that's the first thing I remember when I think of her. Not her face, not her voice—though I remember those things too. But the smell comes first. Vanilla and something sweeter. Maybe honey.

I was four years old. Sitting in the kitchen, swinging my legs on a chair too tall for me. She was making something on the stove—soup, I think. Dad was at work. The afternoon came through the window, golden and warm.

— "Minus, my love, want to taste?"

She bent down with the spoon. Blew to cool it. Her eyes were brown, gentle. They smiled even when her mouth didn't smile.

I tasted it. It was good. I said it was good.

She kissed my forehead.

— "You're my smart boy."

Until that moment, I still hadn't seen the shadows or the illuminated ones. Saw nothing beyond the sunny kitchen, my mother smiling, the smell of vanilla in the air.

In that moment, I was a child, fragile, simple, and happy.

It was only four years. And they were the happiest years of my life, the only truly happy ones.

Until they stopped being.

### MY 9 YEARS OLD

I wake to the tingling.

It always starts like this. Cold fingers on the nape of my neck. Air getting dense. The weight of being watched by things that shouldn't exist.

Nine years old. São Miguel Orphanage. Dormitory with nineteen other children.

And the shadows are back.

I open my eyes slowly. The cracked ceiling of the dormitory stretches above me, stained with humidity. I turn my head—slow movement, controlled, as if not making noise would prevent what's already happening.

They're at the edges. Always start at the edges.

Five. Seven. Ten.

Multiplying like ink spilled in water. Dark shapes writhing against the walls, translucent claws scratching the air. Trying to reach the film around me. That invisible shield separating me from them.

Wrong. Broken. Shouldn't exist.

The whispers come from everywhere and nowhere.

I squeeze my eyes shut. Fingers grip the worn sheet. I know this ritual. The shield will hold. Always holds.

But the price...

Mom smelled like vanilla...

I don't think about it. Not now.

On the other side of the room, Dona Helena snores in her chair. Sixty years old, smells like soap, thinks the shadows are "trials from God." She doesn't see the air vibrating with contained rage. Doesn't feel the storm building.

But I feel it.

That this time will be different—I remember very well.

The shadows aren't just trying to touch me. They're organizing. Forming patterns. A circle around my bed.

The mark on my left shoulder—that spiral scar that appeared the day I lost everything—begins to pulse.

Hot.

Hot as embers.

— “No. Please, not again!”

### THE ILLUMINATED ONE

It appears above the shadows.

Floating. White and golden light so intense it hurts to look directly. Humanoid form, but the contours flicker like candle flame. Something solid underneath. Something with will of its own.

And it looks at me.

Not with protection. Not with mercy.

With hatred.

As if my existence were the colossal error that needs to be erased. As if every breath I take were an insult to the natural order of things.

Dona Helena told me I saw demons. That I should pray more. When I spoke of the Illuminated One, her eyes shone with that certainty only religious adults have.

— "A guardian angel, Minus! It came to protect you!"

But she was wrong.

The Illuminated One protects no one.

It orchestrates.

### MY 9 YEARS OLD (THREE WEEKS AGO)

— "Hey, Minus! Look at this!"

Carlos. Seven years old. Messy hair always falling in his eyes. He ran to my bed with an open notebook, huge smile on his face.

— "I drew Captain America fighting Iron Man! See? I put lasers and everything!"

The drawing was crooked, out of proportion, made with worn colored pencils. Beautiful.

— "It turned out really good," I said.

And it did. It really did.

Carlos beamed. Sat on the edge of my bed, started explaining the whole battle he'd imagined. His eyes danced as he spoke, hands gesturing, creating explosions in the air.

Marina passed by us humming some shower song. Twelve years old, always singing—in the shower, washing dishes, before bed. Off-key voice but happy.

— "You guys are going to wake up Dona Helena," she said, but she was smiling.

Sofia—the little five-year-old—sucked her thumb while hugging a worn stuffed rabbit. She looked at me with those enormous eyes and asked:

— "Minus, do you think I'll get a dad and mom someday?"

— "You will," I said.

I lied.

But she smiled. And in that moment, the lie was worth it.

Three weeks ago. Proof that even in the worst places, we seek happiness. There were good days here.

Until they stopped being.

### THE SHIELD CRACKS

The sound is like glass breaking in slow motion.

CRACK!

Sharp. Impossible. Only I hear it.

Then another.

And another.

Invisible pieces of my shield falling like shards on the floor.

The cracks were always there—fine lines like hair that grew millimeter by millimeter with each new night. Worse on "Special Nights." Like the night Dad stopped breathing, being torn from the inside out, while screaming at me "Wrong! Broken! Shouldn't exist!" and Mom went mad forever. Like the night the Rodrigues' house caught fire, they screamed and smiled as if it were a party.

The cracks never disappear.

Only grow.

But tonight, for the first time, one truly opens.

A gap. Small. The size of a coin.

But open.

The shadows see.

Go mad.

Their forms compress, tear at each other in desperate frenzy. Claws scratching claws. Ethereal bodies crushing trying to pass first. The sound is like a thousand nails on a chalkboard.

I know—with the certainty of someone who's seen this before—that if one of them touches me through that gap, I won't just die.

It will be worse.

They all stop at once.

Raise deformed heads in my direction.

Mouths that don't exist open in obscene smiles.

The Illuminated One raises what should be an arm.

And the shadows disperse.

Don't disappear. Don't flee.

Spread through the dormitory like ink spilled in water. Sliding under beds. Climbing walls. Enveloping sleeping bodies.

Carlos who drew heroes.

Marina who sang.

Sofia who asked if she'd be adopted.

No. Not them. Please, not them.

But it never mattered what I wanted.

### THE MASSACRE

The first scream comes from the bunk beside me.

Carlos.

He wakes with a sound that doesn't seem human. Sharp. Broken. Full of pain no child should ever know.

His hands scratch his own face. Nails digging red furrows. Eyes rolled back showing only white. His artist's hands being used to destroy his own face.

Something dark is coiled around his neck. Not solid. Condensed smoke, pulsing, tightening.

Carlos's skin turns purple.

Then black.

Then—

I can't keep watching.

Marina wakes next. She sits up in bed, rigid, and starts laughing. Hysterical laughter while blood tears stream from her eyes. She sings a malignant song, using strange words. Her hands move on their own, fingers bending at impossible angles.

They snap.

Lucas. Sofia. Pedro. Ana.

One by one.

Nineteen bodies begin to contort.

Dona Helena jumps from her chair, runs to our dormitory, opens the door and screams.

— "What—? Children! What is—?"

Fire appears in the center of the dormitory.

No source. No explanation just like it was at the Rodrigues' house. Just... appears.

A column of red and black flames licking the ceiling. The curtains catch fire. The walls groan.

But the fire is wrong.

Moves against gravity. Against wind. As if alive. The patterns hurt the eyes to look at.

The smell of burning mixes with blood and flesh and something sweet and rotten.

Pedro stands on the bed, head hanging at an impossible angle. Arms twisted backward. Looks like a broken puppet manipulated by invisible strings.

His mouth opens—opens too much—and begins vomiting a black substance that burns holes in the floor.

Sofia is tearing out her own hair. Tufts of skin come with it, revealing pale skull. She screams:

— "Getting pretty for daddy and mommy!"

She laughs.

Everyone is laughing or screaming or doing both at once.

A shadow finds Dona Helena.

Something pierces her chest. Not a blade. Something worse. A claw of solidified darkness that enters through the back and exits through the front.

Blood spurts, black under the light of the flames.

She looks down, confused, before falling to her knees.

### HIDDEN

I pull the sheet over my head.

Curl up. Knees against chest. Hands covering ears.

It doesn't help.

I still hear the wet sounds. The cracking of bones. The tearing of flesh. The dripping of things that should be inside bodies.

I still feel the heat of fire. The cold of shadows. The weight of the Illuminated One watching me through the thin sheet.

I still smell iron and burned flesh and fear.

— "No," I whisper in the dark. Voice broken, childish, useless. "Please, no. I don't want this. I didn't ask for this."

Mom smelled like vanilla.

Carlos drew heroes.

Marina sang.

Sofia asked about daddies and mommies.

Hell doesn't negotiate. Doesn't apologize. Doesn't ask permission.

It just comes.

Glass breaks. Wood cracks. Someone starts praying out loud, words stumbling over each other until they become just sounds.

And above it all, bathing the massacre in golden and impassive light, the Illuminated One floats.

Watching.

Always watching.

This is your fault.

The voice—not a voice, a feeling—whispers directly into my brain.

Every scream. Every death. Every piece of torn flesh.

All your fault.

Always was.

Always will be.

The mark on my shoulder burns like red-hot iron. Tears soak the sheet that hides me from nothing.

Nineteen children slept in this dormitory when the night began.

Now I hear fewer screams.

Fewer and fewer.

Until only the crackling of fire remains. The constant dripping on the floor. The heavy silence that comes after.

— "I'm sorry," I whisper to those who can no longer hear me. "I'm sorry. I'm sorry. I'm sorry."

But apologies don't bring anyone back to life.

### THE DAWN

When the sun rises over São Miguel Orphanage, when firefighters and police arrive, when they find the twisted bodies and burned walls and impossible patterns of blood on the ceiling...

They have questions.

— How did the fire start without a source?

— How did children do that to each other?

— How did you survive when nineteen died?

When they find me—alive, unharmed, hidden under a sheet in the center of the massacre—I see it in their faces.

Suspicion. Fear. Horror. Hatred. Pain.

All those feelings pointed at me like knives.

— What did you do?

— What ARE you?

The truth—that there are shadows and light, that there's a shield cracking, that there's something wrong with me since the day I was born—is something no one would believe.

And even if they believed...

It wouldn't save anyone.

Under the sheet stained with ashes and tears, I wait.

This was the third time.

And deep in my nine-year-old heart, I already know a truth that weighs more than any accusation:

It won't be the last.

Hell always finds me.

And always brings company.

### NARRATOR: FLAW

I keep watching.

Even when the impure ones take him. Even when they put him in a white room with questions he can't answer. Even when they look at him as if he were a monster.

I keep watching.

Because I've seen some marked ones like him, but none of them ever looked back.

But Minus looked.

And in the infinitesimal second when that thing touched him, in the moment the mark formed, he saw something even I can't see.

Something that might explain why I exist.

And as long as this mystery remains unanswered, as long as the question burns inside my flawed and purposeless existence...

I keep watching.

Because this is what I do.

Always.
