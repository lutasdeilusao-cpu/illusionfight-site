# CHAPTER 3 — CHAINS

### THE AWAKENING · NARRATOR: MINUS

The shadows retreat.

Don't flee. Don't disappear. Just... stop.

As if something had changed. As if the rules they'd known for centuries had just broken.

I'm standing. Legs shaking. The cord between me and Flaw pulses with silver light that hurts to look at.

For the first time since I was born, the shadows don't immediately attack me.

They hesitate.

And then the sky changes.

That Shadow World sky—black storms, twisted clouds, purple lightning that hurts the soul—begins to crack.

Not crack like glass. Crack like flesh.

Fissures of light open. Golden. Blinding. Completely wrong in this place of darkness.

The Illuminated Ones.

But they don't cross over.

They stay on the other side. In the real world. Looking through the cracks like someone peeking through door slits.

And even from afar, even through the barriers between worlds, I feel it.

Hatred.

Pure and crystalline hatred directed at me.

They want me to die. Want the shadows to destroy me. But they won't enter here to do the job themselves.

This world is too corrupt even for them.

The light leaking through the fissures isn't protection. Isn't salvation.

It's an order.

And the shadows—all of them—turn their deformed heads to the sky.

Receiving instructions.

Then they start moving again.

Slowly at first. Then faster. Forming a circle around me.

No longer hesitant.

Obedient.

— "Shit."

### NARRATOR: FLAW

I see when he understands.

The Illuminated Ones won't save him. Never wanted to. Never will.

To them, Minus is the error. The broken one. What must be corrected.

And "corrected" means erased.

The shadows advance. I feel through the cord—his fear, his confusion, his growing despair.

But I feel something else too.

Anger.

Small. Fragile. But growing.

And along with the anger, something deeper. A spark of understanding.

The rules are different here.

In the world of the impure, he was weak. Vulnerable. Just a child running from forces he didn't understand.

But here...

Here, the Shadow World responds to him in ways even he doesn't know.

I see when he looks at the pulsing flesh floor. At the breathing walls. At the cracked sky leaking sickly light.

And then I see when he bends his knees.

And jumps.

### THE LEAP · NARRATOR: MINUS

I don't think. Just do.

I bend my knees—legs burning with tension—and jump.

I expect to go maybe half a meter. Just enough to get away from the nearest shadows.

I go much farther.

The world explodes in movement. Dense air whips my face. The ground falls away too fast. My stomach stays behind.

I rise.

And rise.

And rise.

Five meters. Ten. Fifteen.

I pass over the roof of the house next door. Over the electrical wires glowing with purple, wrong energy. Over everything.

And for a second—a single second suspended in air—I see the entire Shadow World stretching below.

The distorted city. The streets of flesh. The houses with windows that are open mouths screaming silently. The cracked sky leaking light from the watching Illuminated Ones.

And the shadows.

Thousands of them. Maybe millions. Infesting every corner, every alley, every shadow projected in this inverted world.

All looking up.

All seeing me.

Then gravity returns.

I fall.

Arms flailing in the air. Trying to balance. Don't know how to land. Don't know how to stop.

I hit a wall—no, pass through it. The structure falls apart like wet paper. I roll across the floor of a house. Table. Chairs. Everything rotten and pulsing.

I get up stumbling.

Flaw is with me. Always with me. Like my own shadow stuck to my back. The cord keeps us united, pulsing with silver light.

I hear sounds behind. Scratching. Growling.

They're coming.

I run.

### THE PURSUIT

My feet pound the flesh floor. Each step makes a wet, disgusting sound. I breathe dense air that tastes like iron and rot.

I turn a corner. Vaguely recognize it—it's the street where Mr. Jorge has his newsstand.

But here, the stand is twisted mass of wood and flesh fused. The magazines are pages of skin. The headlines written with blood that never dries.

I run past.

Something grabs me.

Translucent claw pierces my shoulder. Pain explodes—not physical, worse. As if the claw had touched something inside me that shouldn't be touched.

I scream.

The creature—bipedal, tall, faceless head—pulls me. I try to break free. Can't.

Another comes from the right. This one has many arms. All ending in claws. All wanting me.

Flaw feels my pain through the cord. I feel her wanting to help. But she doesn't know how.

She never had to fight before.

Only observe.

The claw tightens. Tears. Blood—my blood—runs warm down my arm.

I struggle. Useless.

More shadows join. Five. Ten. Twenty.

All wanting to touch me. Tear me. Destroy me.

I punch one. My fist passes through it like smoke. Doesn't hurt.

They laugh. Wet and wrong sound.

One knocks me down. I hit the ground. The flesh beneath me pulses like giant heart.

They approach.

Surrounding.

Savoring.

And then I see it.

Through the alley. Dragging on three legs.

A canine shadow.

Smaller than the others. Torn ear. Missing paw.

Exactly like Shadow.

Exactly like my dog.

### SHADOW'S SHADOW · NARRATOR: MINUS

— "No..."

The voice comes out broken. Childish.

The shadow-dog approaches. But not to help me. Not to protect me.

To destroy me.

Like all the others.

Its eyes—two red points where eyes should be—fix on me. Mouth opens. There's no tongue. Only void. Void full of teeth.

It growls.

Shadow never growled at me.

Not once in six months.

But this... this thing using his form... growls as if it hated me.

— "You're not him," I whisper. Tears burn my eyes. "You're not my boy."

The shadow-dog advances.

The other shadows stop. Watching. Savoring my suffering.

Through the cord, I feel Flaw. Her confusion. Her... sadness?

She feels what I feel.

And what I feel is complete destruction.

My dog died protecting me. Gave his life so I could run.

And now this world took his form and turned it into a weapon against me.

The shadow-dog jumps.

I close my eyes.

And then I hear it.

Not with ears. Inside my head.

A voice that isn't voice. Words that aren't words.

But I understand anyway.

### FIRST MESSAGE · NARRATOR: FLAW

I've never spoken before.

Never had reason.

Observing was enough. Drifting between worlds was enough.

But now—seeing Minus surrounded, hurt, looking at that corruption of the creature he loved—I feel something break inside me.

I can't just observe.

Not anymore.

Through the cord, I project. Not sound. Not speech. Something more direct.

Silver smoke materializes between Minus and the shadow-dog. Condenses. Forms something like a screen—translucent, glowing, impossible.

And on it, using the essence of my own existence, I write:

— TRANSFORM YOUR PRISON INTO YOUR POWER

The words shine. Pulse. Not in language he knows, but in pure meaning that goes straight to his brain.

The shadows don't see the screen. Don't perceive the penumbra.

But they feel.

Feel the power emanating from it like a wave of impossible pressure.

Something they don't comprehend. Something that makes them hesitate.

They become apprehensive. Uncertain.

Minus opens his eyes. Looks at the screen. At the words.

I see when understanding begins to form.

"Prison?" he whispers.

### THE MEMORIES · NARRATOR: MINUS

And then I feel it.

Don't see. Don't hear. Feel.

As if something inside me—something buried for thirteen years—were waking.

The memories don't come from Flaw. Come from me. From inside me.

### Mom gone mad. Empty eyes. Smiling while Dad bled on the floor.

### The blue shield appearing. Enveloping. Protecting.

### But with protection came isolation. Loneliness. The weight of being different.

### They took this from me, I suddenly realize. The Illuminated Ones. The shadows. Each piece that broke... they stole.

### Orphanage. Children dying. Carlos. Marina. Sofia.

CRACK. CRACK. CRACK.

### Pieces torn away. Stolen. Taken by forces that hated me.

### The Rodrigues family. Flames. Screams. Insane laughter.

CRACK.

### More pieces taken from me.

### The massacre at São Miguel. Nineteen bodies. Nineteen pieces torn away while I just survived.

CRACK. CRACK. CRACK.

### Four years on the streets. Five random deaths. People touched by shadows near me.

CRACK.

### And finally—Shadow. My dog. My only friend.

### The claw piercing. The scream. The fall.

CRACK.

The last big piece torn from me.

All those cracks. All those fragments.

They didn't disappear.

Were stolen. Taken. Used against me.

But they're still mine.

Always were mine.

And now...

"It's time to take them back," I whisper.

On the smoke screen, new words appear:

— CALL

I close my eyes.

I feel the pieces. Don't see, but know where they are. Floating between worlds. Suspended. Waiting.

I never needed a shield.

Shields only protect. Only isolate. Only separate.

I need something more.

I need something to fight with. To defend myself. To attack.

I need a weapon.

And I extend my hand.

Not physical. Deeper.

I extend through the mark on my shoulder. Through the spiral scars covering my arms.

And I call.

Come back to me.

### RETURN OF THE FRAGMENTS

It happens slowly at first.

A point of blue light appears in the air. Small. Size of a fingernail.

Then another.

And another.

The shadows notice. Retreat slightly. Confused.

More points appear. Dozens. Hundreds. All blue and bright. All fragments of what they stole from me.

Each one carrying memory. Each one marked by loss.

Carlos who drew heroes.

Marina who sang.

Sofia who asked if she'd be adopted.

The Rodrigues burning.

Mom gone mad.

Dad bleeding.

Shadow falling.

Everything they took from me. Everything they used to break me.

Returning.

They float around me. Orbiting. Waiting.

The mark on my shoulder burns. Doesn't hurt. Recognizes.

This was always mine.

The fragments begin to move. Not randomly. With purpose.

They gravitate to the mark. To the spiral scars.

And begin to stick.

The first touches my left shoulder—where the mark pulses. Sticks like magnet. Then another. And another.

They coil. Forming line. Forming chain.

Luminous blue descending down my arm. Wrapping my bicep. My elbow. My wrist.

Then they climb. Cross my chest. Wrap my heart.

Each link a recovered memory. Each fragment a piece of me taken back.

The chain pulses. Alive. Part of me but separate. Weapon and extension.

Through the cord, I feel Flaw. She's feeding this. Her power flowing to me. My power flowing to her.

Transformation happening between us.

And slowly—so slowly—I begin to stand.

### CHAINS MANIFEST · NARRATOR: MINUS

The chain finishes forming.

It's not pure blue. Small black points—like ink—begin to appear. Dripping from Flaw through the cord. Mixing with the luminous blue.

Creating something new.

Something that isn't just mine. Isn't just hers.

Ours.

I stand completely. Legs firm. The weight of the chain should bring me down—each link the size of a fist, solid, real.

But it doesn't weigh.

Feels like natural extension. Like it should always have been there.

The shadows attack.

The first—tall bipedal creature—advances with claws extended.

I don't think. My arm moves.

The chain responds.

Leaves my shoulder like a serpent. Fast. Precise. Coils around the creature.

Tightens.

The shadow screeches. Sharp and wrong sound. Tries to dissolve into smoke. Escape.

The chain tightens more.

And then—with sound like imploding glass—the creature explodes.

Not blood. Not flesh.

Black smoke. Pure and simple. Dissipating in the air.

Existence erased.

The others stop.

Look at me differently.

No longer as prey.

As threat.

### NARRATOR: FLAW

I feel when he kills the first one.

The sensation crosses the cord. Power going out. Power coming back. Complete cycle.

And inside me—somewhere I didn't know existed—I feel something awaken.

Not just observer.

Witness.

Throughout the ages, I saw the Illuminated Ones try in every way to exterminate the shadows. Saw battles. Saw massacres. Saw light against darkness in eternal conflict.

And the shadows were never eliminated.

Never.

But now...

Now I see a shadow being destroyed. Erased. Ceasing to exist.

And not by an Illuminated One.

By an impure one.

By a marked one.

By a broken one.

Minus—the boy who looked back—is killing shadows in a way that neither light nor darkness could.

And suddenly it begins to make sense.

Why the Illuminated Ones hate the marked ones.

Why the shadows want to destroy them.

Somehow—somehow I still don't fully understand—the broken ones have power to stop both.

To destroy both.

To end the eternal war.

And that...

That is unacceptable to both sides.

The shadows come. Fulfilling orders from the Illuminated Ones watching from above.

And for the first time since I've existed, I don't just observe the battle.

I begin to understand.

### THE BATTLE · NARRATOR: MINUS

They come from all sides.

Crawling on walls. Jumping from roofs. Emerging from the flesh floor.

Two dozen. Maybe more.

The chain moves. Sometimes by my will. Sometimes on its own.

As if it had consciousness of its own.

Or maybe it's Flaw. Our power mixed. Indistinguishable.

A shadow leaps from the left—quadrupedal form, fast as a dog.

The chain whips. Catches it in the air. Smashes against wall.

The wall cracks. Rotten flesh explodes. The house groans.

Two come from the right. Tall. Humanoid. Claws like blades.

I jump—and again, I go higher than I should. The rules are different here.

I use the chain as whip. It extends. Catches both. Pulls.

They fly. Hit each other. Explode into smoke.

More come.

Always more.

I run through the distorted street. The chain leaves trail of blue and black light. Each movement destroys something.

A post falls. The sickly lights flicker and die.

A storefront explodes. Not glass—something worse. Something organic.

The ground cracks under my feet. Flesh opens. I see something pulsing underneath. Don't want to know what it is.

The shadow-dog comes.

Faster than the others. Knows my movements because it was Shadow's.

Jumps at my chest.

I fall.

Teeth try for my neck. Bite. Don't pierce but burn.

— "GET OFF!" I shout. Tears in my eyes. "You're not him! NOT HIM!"

The chain hesitates.

Because it's Shadow. Or was. Or something like it.

And part of me doesn't want to hurt him.

The creature bites again. Harder.

But I can't.

Can't destroy the last image of him.

Even if it's corruption. Even if it's shadow. Even if it's not really him.

It's all I have left.

"I'm sorry," I whisper. Not to the creature. To the real Shadow. Wherever he is. "I'm sorry I wasn't strong enough."

The chain moves. But not to destroy.

To capture.

Coils around the shadow-dog. Gentle but firm. Trapping. Containing.

The creature struggles. Screeches. Tries to bite. Tries to scratch.

But the chain holds.

And I hold.

Because even being shadow. Even being corruption.

It's all I have of him.

And I won't let go.

### DESTRUCTION

The other shadows retreat.

Finally.

Not because they're afraid. Because they receive new order.

The fissures in the sky shine brighter. Golden light leaking. The Illuminated Ones changing tactics.

If the shadows can't kill me directly...

If I can't kill all of them...

The Shadow World itself will try.

The ground begins to shake. Not earthquake. Worse.

As if the entire world were waking. Realizing it has intruder.

Buildings begin to collapse. Not brick and concrete—flesh and bone. Impossible structures of living matter collapsing in on themselves.

The street opens. Enormous cracks showing void below. Void full of eyes. Thousands of red eyes blinking.

I run.

The shadow-dog trapped in the chain, being dragged with me. It struggles. Screams. Sound that hurts the ears.

But I don't let go.

The chain seeks anchor. Grabs onto post. Onto traffic light. Pulls me forward.

Like metallic spider web. Swinging me through the destruction.

The shadow-dog comes along. Trapped. Captured.

Behind us, the Shadow World falls apart. Piece by piece.

Because I fought. Because I killed shadows. Because I used power I shouldn't have.

The balance was broken.

The chain grabs onto something solid. Don't see what. Doesn't matter.

It pulls me. Hard. Like hook in the chest.

And suddenly I'm flying. Not upward.

Outward.

Through the membrane between worlds. Through my own projected shadow.

Back.

Taking with me the last thing remaining of my friend.

### RETURN

I hit hard concrete.

Real air—true air without taste of iron—fills my lungs.

I roll. Hit something. Tree. Solid. Alive. Normal.

I stay there. Breathing. Each breath hurts.

The real world around me. Sounds of sirens in the distance. Smell of asphalt and gasoline. Normal streetlights. Yellow. Not sickly.

So beautiful I want to cry.

I lean against the tree. Slide down to sit. Legs folded.

The chain is still here. Coiled around my arm. Around my chest. Solid and real.

And trapped in it...

The shadow-dog.

It crossed over with me. Left the Shadow World. It's here. In the real world.

More solid than it should be. More real. Black and distorted canine form. Red eyes still glowing. But trapped. Contained.

It growls. Low. But doesn't attack.

The chain holds it.

"Shhh," I whisper. Hand extended—slow, careful. "Quiet, boy."

The shadow-dog trembles. Confused. This isn't its world. Shouldn't be here.

But it is.

Because I brought it.

Because I couldn't let go.

My hand touches its head. I expect pain. Expect burning.

But through the chain—through the power that unites us—I feel only...

Echo.

Distant echo of something that was love. Of something that was loyalty.

Corrupted. Broken. But still there.

— "You're not him," I whisper. Tears flow. "But you're all I have."

The shadow-dog stops growling.

And then I see her.

### THE QUESTION · NARRATOR: MINUS

Flaw is in front of me.

No longer connected by the cord. That dissolved when I crossed back over.

But she's here. Manifest in a way I've never seen.

Not just penumbra. Not just presence.

Almost human form. Feminine. Made of silver smoke and liquid darkness. Hair floating as if underwater. Eyes—two points of cold light—watching me.

Always watching me.

But different now.

As if she'd seen something new. Something that changed her.

She looks at me. At the shadow-dog trapped in the chain. And back at me.

Doesn't judge. Just observes.

As she always has.

I look at her. Really look. For the first time in thirteen years.

And I ask the only thing that matters:

— "What's your name?"

She doesn't speak. Not with voice.

But the smoke screen appears. Between us. Glowing softly.

And on it, written with the essence of her own existence:

— YES

I blink. Confused.

— "Yes? Yes what?"

She keeps looking at me. And then I understand.

She wasn't answering my question.

She was confirming something deeper.

Yes, I have a name.

Yes, you can know it.

Yes, this changes everything.

The screen changes. New words form:

— FLAW

I read. Reread.

— "Flaw," I whisper. Testing the name. Feeling it on my tongue.

She doesn't move. Just observes.

As she always has.

But now I know. Now I understand.

I'm not alone.

I never was.

I lean better against the tree. Body aching. Chain weighing on my arm. The shadow-dog coiled at my feet, trembling, confused, but contained.

And for the first time in thirteen years, since Shadow died, since everything fell apart...

I smile.

Small. Broken.

But real.

— "Pleasure to meet you, Flaw."

### NARRATOR: FLAW

He smiles.

For the first time since I started watching him—through all the deaths, all the tragedies, all the pieces stolen from him...

Minus smiles.

And something inside me—in this void where existence should be—warms.

I look at the shadow-dog at his feet. Impossible creature. Shouldn't exist here. In the world of the impure.

But it does.

Because he couldn't let go.

Because even broken, even being the error, even being hated by light and darkness...

He can still love.

And maybe—just maybe—that's what I lack understanding.

The boy who looked back.

The boy who transformed prison into power.

The boy who brought shadow into light.

The boy who finally asked my name.

I keep watching.

Always.

But now it's not all I do.

Now I'm part.

And that—I will discover—changes absolutely everything.
