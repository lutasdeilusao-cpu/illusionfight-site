# CHAPTER 8 — The Basement

**JACK**

I waited.

Lit a cigarette. Let the bullets fly.

Went up a few steps, to get away from ricochets, and stayed in a covered corner, behind a column. Smoking. Counting.

Until I heard the guns go empty. Click. Click. Click.

---

Then I moaned out loud.

— Oh my God… what the fuck… this is blood…

Primetime soap opera acting. Pretended I'd taken a bullet.

The first one bought it. Came to check, slow, empty gun in his hand.

Took a kick to the knee that bent his leg the wrong way.

---

I grabbed him by the neck before he fell and put him in front of my body. Human shield.

The others were lost. Reload? Don't reload? Shoot their own buddy?

While they were thinking, I threw the shield onto one of them.

And I threw the pipe at the one who was already turning his gun on me. Caught him right in the stomach. The guy folded.

---

The third one was armed too.

I ducked just enough. Two shots went over.

I spun, put my back to the guy and grabbed the gun arm.

Bent his hand the way hands bend. Only I kept going. Kept going till the hand touched the forearm. And it broke.

---

I didn't let go.

Still with my back to him, I pulled the guy close, wrapped my arm behind his head and turned my body.

The guy did a hundred and eighty degrees in the air and landed on his back on the floor, in front of me.

I didn't even look down. Looking to the side, I stomped on his chest.

Stomped again.

Till I heard the rib crack.

---

That's when two more showed up from the back.

Both had more fear in their faces than fight.

Problem is, Kim had made the rule real clear upstairs. Wanted to fight or not, now they were gonna.

— Easy, bro, easy. Wait, wait…

---

I grabbed one by the neck.

The other one, I gave a spinning backfist.

*Whack.*

Caught him right in the throat. The guy started losing air.

---

The one by the neck took jabs to the solar plexus. One after another, same hand.

Pow, pow, pow, pow, pow.

The guy went down to his knees. I kept going.

Pow, pow, pow, pow, pow.

When I noticed, the guy was already out. It wasn't the punches that dropped him. It was the air running out.

I let go.

---

The other one was still choking, hand on his throat.

I pulled his face into my knee.

The blood came down.

---

Everybody was on the floor. Nobody looking like they'd get up.

But a deal's a deal. So I kept going. Making sure, one by one, none of them would get up. That none of them would become a problem for Kim upstairs.

The basement was ours.

---

Before going up, obviously, I fixed my hair. Some things aren't negotiable.

**KIM**

He fixed his hair in the middle of a basement full of unconscious people.

**JACK**

Priorities, Kim.

Then I went shopping.

Found a tonfa, that nightstick with the side handle, the one you can run flat along your forearm the way Kim uses the baton. Took it.

Found a chain. Wrapped it around my hand.

Stuffed a few little baggies of powder in my pocket.

**KIM**

To this day I don't know why.

**JACK**

And I found a crate of mini Cokes. Those little glass bottles, about 8 ounces.

Tied a piece of string around the neck of each one and made a belt out of them around my waist. Glass bottles clinking against each other with every step.

---

I looked at myself in the reflection of a dirty window. Fixed my hair one more time.

— Tonight's gonna be a long night.

And I went up.
