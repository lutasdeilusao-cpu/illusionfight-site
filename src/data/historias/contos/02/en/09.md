# CHAPTER 9 — The Panther

Taking on that kid was never just about honor.

I needed him out of my way, any way possible, as fast as possible. If a slap would do it, great. If it took more, fine too. That's how my first fight with the Kid started.

The first of many.

---

The first thing I noticed was that he dropped into a stance.

Feet in the right place, hands at the right height, weight balanced. Somebody had taught him that, or he'd copied it off somebody — no way to tell. A kid that size, half my age, and still his body already knew something mine took years and a lot of beatings to learn.

I went at him careless. Wasn't taking it seriously.

I paid for it.

He came in fast, and the hook he landed in my stomach made it clear the slap-and-bye plan wasn't going to work. It hurt for real. If he'd put more weight into it, turned his hips more, used the drive from his legs, maybe that first punch would've really backed me up.

But he didn't have that yet. He had the idea and not the experience. He had the knack and not the technique. You buy those things with time.

What he had was something else. Something you can't buy.

---

My counter just grazed him. Just enough to open the tiniest gap for him to land a kick on me.

From there on I got serious.

I couldn't waste time, so I wanted to finish it fast. Harder shots. An elbow here, a knee there. And from the moment I got serious, he didn't lay another finger on me.

But something strange happened.

The more I hit him and he couldn't hit back, the more his style changed. At the start it had been technical — tight, economical, not one wasted strike. After that he started going wild. Got lower. Put his hands on the ground. Looked like a small animal looking up in my direction.

A panther cub.

The more blood I drew from him, the wilder the panther got. No technique at all now. Just wanting to land something.

---

And there was the other problem.

That kid had a toughness that wasn't normal. I didn't see it coming. I'd dropped grown men with less work, and there I was, beating on a child who refused to go down.

It had been a long time since I'd had to take anybody seriously. Still, with all the difference in size, in age, in everything — the kid was still going to have to eat a whole lot of rice and beans to get anywhere near me in a fight.

The fight ended with a knee right in the middle of his face.

He stayed standing. Bleeding. Wrecked. We both already knew that was the end.

But he didn't fall.

The damn kid wouldn't fall. And I didn't have any more time for it — I needed to get those two kilos as far away from there as possible. So I left the kid standing in the middle of the street, refusing to fall, and walked off.

---

Some other day, some other situation, maybe I would've used that time to recruit.

A kid who won't go down is worth gold on the Hill.

But I was in a hurry. I thought that was the end of it. I thought I'd never see that boy again in my life.

Big mistake. Big mistake on my part.
