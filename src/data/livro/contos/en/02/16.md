# CHAPTER 16 — No Rules

That Monday morning I climbed up to the rooftop and the two of them were already there waiting for me.

Before anything, I spoke.

— Listen up. This is the last one. After today you're not gonna find me anymore. Don't ask why.

Jack opened his mouth to crack a joke and, for the first time in two years, shut it again without saying anything. The Kid just clenched his jaw.

They understood it was serious.

---

— Before we start, there's something I never asked you — I said. — We've been meeting for two years and I never knew your names. To me you're the Kid and Green Hair. What are they for real?

The Kid answered like he was spitting out his own name.

— Kim.

The green hair took a step forward, the same old grin.

— My name's Jack. — He paused. — Well, my name's not Jack. But this little guy here calls me Jack, so it counts.

— You already know mine. I'm Alan. — I nodded. — There. Now it's official.

Kim. Jack. Alan.

After two years, we finally had names for each other.

---

— Since this is the last time, I'm gonna make one thing clear.

I walked to the middle of the rooftop. Took the chain off my neck and put it in my pocket.

— Every other time I fought you, I was playing. Not today. Today I'm gonna face you like my life is on the table. Like everything I built depends on this fight. I'm gonna bet it all — every drop of blood I ever bled, every beating I took to get where I got, every single thing that hurt along the way. Today you're gonna see the real me. Like you never have.

The two of them exchanged a look.

— Take it seriously from the first second. Come with everything from the first second. Otherwise you're gonna get hurt for real today.

---

— And I'm gonna teach you the only rule of street fighting — I said. — There's just one. You know what it is?

They looked at each other again, kind of confused, searching for the right answer.

Mistake.

— THERE ARE NO RULES.

I was already on top of Jack before I finished yelling.

All my weight back, hips turned to the bone, fist coming up from below. An uppercut to the chin with everything I had. Jack left the ground. Went up in the air, spun, and landed on his back knocked out before he'd even finished falling.

Two years and I'd never seen Jack on the ground. Kim hadn't either.

And Kim made the same mistake the two of them had just made: he looked at his friend.

I stomped on his foot to pin it and buried a knee in his solar plexus. All his air came out at once. He flew backwards, folded over, and I was already coming in for the next shot.

---

Kim was on the ground, no air, trying to land a kick on my chin any way he could.

I dodged. And stopped.

Because the kid looking up at me from down there wasn't the same anymore.

Something changed inside him in that second. When he saw Jack knocked out on the ground, a switch flipped. The look of an arrogant boy was gone. In its place there was a cold look, still, of somebody who wasn't going to hold anything back anymore.

And for the first time in a long time, I felt fear.

---

Kim kept that line. Years later he was going to repeat it, word for word, to a guy who'd hired half a dozen goons ([main story, ch. 3](/historias/lutas-de-ilusao/capitulo-03)). I doubt he remembers where he got it.
