# CHAPTER 9 — The Kitchen

To get to the kitchen, I had to cross the whole party going against traffic.

One came, I dodged. Another came, I dropped him. By then it wasn't just the entourage: people I'd never seen were fighting people who'd never seen me. That's how party brawls go. They start with a reason and keep going because everybody's already on their feet.

I shoved the kitchen door open with my shoulder.

There was a couple making out on top of the counter. They both stopped, looked at me, looked at the door, heard the yelling outside.

— What's going on? — she asked.

— Opposition scheming. Carry on.

They carried on.

---

The cake was on the table. Three tiers, white frosting, strawberries on top. A work of art.

When I went for the knife, a girl came in through the other door and charged at me with a metal tray raised over her head.

— YOU'RE THE ONE WHO STARTED IT!

Kim and I have a rule: man, woman, old-timer or kid — you step onto the court, you'd better be ready to play.

But I had a cake to cut and no time to play. Besides, she was a lovely young lady. No reason to leave a mark on that pretty face.

So I decided to dance. I caught the tray in midair, turned her around slowly and spun her with me.

— Whoa, whoa, easy, miss. Let's dance.

Two little steps this way, two that way. She got so confused she forgot about the tray. I sat her down on a stool, grabbed the knife and cut a generous slice.

One slice. Not the whole cake. I have principles: it was the guy's birthday. He'd want to eat that cake. Freddy threw one hell of a party — the least bit of consideration I could show him was leaving him the rest of the cake.

I put it on a plate and left.

---

Meanwhile, at the buffet table.

Freddy had arrived. He was in front of Kim, face an inch from his, spitting as he yelled:

— WHERE'S YOUR FRIEND? WHERE'S THAT SON OF A BITCH?

Kim chewed. Looked at him. Chewed.

Then Kim's right hand started to close.

And stopped halfway.

*Don't touch the blond.*

Kim opened his hand again, picked up a little pastry and kept chewing. That made Freddy angrier than any punch ever could have.
