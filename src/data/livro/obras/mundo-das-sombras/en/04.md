# CHAPTER 4 — NEFELI

### NARRATOR: MINUS

— "Pleasure to meet you, Flaw."

The words still echo in the air between us. She keeps watching me with those cold light eyes, motionless as she's always been.

My body hurts. Every breath burns. I feel dried blood sticking to the torn shirt. My left shoulder pulses where the claw pierced me in the Shadow World.

But I'm alive.

And I'm not alone.

My eyes drop to the creature at my feet.

The shadow-dog is coiled in the chain—trapped, contained, but alive in an impossible way. The red eyes blink. Not with recognition. With hunger. With wild and primitive rage.

This isn't the Shadow that followed me. That slept beside me. That shared bread with me.

This is something different. Condensed violence. Destruction with canine form.

And even knowing this... I extend my hand.

Slowly. Very slowly.

The creature growls. Low. Threatening. The teeth—translucent, sharp, wrong—appear between lips that shouldn't exist.

— "Quiet," I whisper. Voice hoarse from screaming.

My hand approaches. The shadow-dog shrinks back. Ready to attack. Ready to bite. Ready to destroy.

I touch its head.

The creature lunges—mouth open, teeth seeking my fingers—but the chain tightens. Gentle. Firm. Holding.

The teeth stop millimeters from my skin.

And I don't withdraw my hand.

— "Quiet, boy," I repeat. Firmer now.

The shadow-dog trembles. Confusion crosses those impossible eyes. As if two natures were fighting inside it.

My fingers caress between the translucent ears. One of them torn. Exactly like the real Shadow's.

— "You're not him," I whisper. "But you'll have to do."

The creature stops growling. Not from calm. From confusion.

And in that moment, something inside me—something broken since Shadow fell—mends a little.

Not completely. Never completely.

But enough.

### THE SKY

I withdraw my hand. The shadow-dog just stays there, trembling, trapped in the chain, existing somewhere between worlds.

I look up.

The night sky. Stars. Crescent moon. Normal. Beautiful.

But at the edges—always at the edges—I feel them.

The Illuminated Ones.

Two came to observe tonight. Now only one remains, floating above my head, trying to understand why I'm still alive.

And for the first time—for the first time since everything began—I see movement.

The head turns. Slowly.

One. Two. Three seconds.

The luminous form—humanoid but not human—turns completely in my direction.

And looks at me.

Not passive observation. Direct recognition. Pure hatred.

And for the first time... something that could be fear.

Its arm raises. The hand closes.

You are the error that must be corrected.

My body screams pain. Every muscle. Every bone. Swollen eye. Dried blood. Cracked ribs.

I'm destroyed.

But something inside me—something that survived thirteen years of hell—refuses to accept.

I stand.

Slowly. Staggering. Using the tree for support.

The chain weighs on my arm. Solid. Real. Mine.

Each link a recovered memory. Each fragment a piece of me I took back.

Carlos who drew heroes.

Marina who sang.

Sofia who asked if she'd be adopted.

Shadow who died protecting me.

All there. All with me. Always.

My right arm rises. The chain slides. Blue and black mixed.

My fingers close. All of them. Except one.

And looking directly at that Illuminated One—at that golden light that destroyed my life...

I show the middle finger.

Obscene gesture. Human. Simple.

And somehow more powerful than any supernatural power.

Fuck you too.

The golden light pulses. Once. Strong. Pure rage.

But doesn't descend. Doesn't attack.

Because it knows—the same way I know—that something changed.

I'm no longer prey running.

I'm threat.

The light begins to disappear. Retreating. To the edges. To observe.

To plan.

And I stay there. Standing. Bleeding. Broken.

But alive.

With a penumbra at my side and an impossible shadow-dog at my feet.

And for the first time since my father's death and my mother's madness...

I smile for real.

### FOUR YEARS LATER · NARRATOR: NEFELI

My name is no longer Flaw.

Three months after that night, when the wounds healed, Minus gave me a new name.

Nefeli. From Greek. Cloud.

Because I am mist. Because I exist between worlds. Because I'm neither shadow nor light.

And for the first time, I didn't feel just like an error.

I felt like a choice.

I watch Minus now—seventeen years old, no longer the thirteen-year-old boy breaking under the weight of the world.

The electric blue dread mohawk sways as he walks down W3 Avenue in Brasília. The color perfectly matches the blue of the chain when it manifests.

Not by accident.

He learned to own what was stolen from him.

Torn jeans. Steel chain on the belt. Fingerless gloves hiding the spiral marks. Faded black t-shirt. Short sleeveless overcoat.

Each piece a declaration.

Look. See what I survived. And I'm still here.

Four years searching. For others like him. Other marked ones. Other broken ones.

We found two before this one.

Both left—never knowing Minus was near, but feeling something wrong. Their shields reacting to a threat they couldn't identify.

But this time...

### THE ENCOUNTER · NARRATOR: MINUS

She appears out of nowhere.

Girl—brown-skinned, light greenish eyes—"stumbles" in front of me.

Classic pickpocket.

Bumps my shoulder. Light. Professional. Fingers already searching for wallet, phone, anything.

But my chain is faster.

Invisible to her, coils around the wrist. Not squeezing. Just blocking.

She freezes. Fraction of a second. Feels something but doesn't know what.

I keep walking.

— "HEY!" The voice explodes behind me. "What do you think you're doing? You can't do that to a girl!"

Some people stop. Look.

I don't turn. Don't accelerate. Just continue.

I hear when she picks up the can from the ground. Crushed soda can.

I hear the metallic sound cutting the air.

I move my neck. Minimal. Precise.

The can passes exactly where my head was.

And in that movement—in that second when the chain protected me instinctively—I see.

On her shoulder. Through the worn denim jacket.

The light. Pulsing. In spiral.

Mark.

She's marked.

I keep walking. A group of people crosses between us.

I use the second of cover.

The chain pulls me. Between two people. Between car and post. Sliding like ghost.

When she looks again... I've disappeared.

### THE ROOFTOP · NARRATOR: MINUS

I appear on the rooftop three blocks ahead. Nefeli is beside me.

Almost-human form. Mist hair floating. Silver eyes fixed on me.

— "So?" I ask.

The screen materializes:

— MARKED. NO DOUBT

I shake my head. "Three in four years."

— THEY LEFT BEFORE

— "Felt I was different," I say. "Even without seeing me. Their shields reacted to me."

Silence. Then:

— WHAT DO YOU WANT TO DO?

I lean on the edge. Brasília stretches below.

— "She tried to rob me. Difficult situation. Like mine was. On the streets, probably."

— YOU WANT TO HELP HER

— "I want to observe her," I correct. "See if the Illuminated Ones and shadows act the same with other marked ones. Or if it's just me. I need to understand, Nefeli."

— AND RED EYES?

I look down. Three blocks south, I see the familiar red glow.

— "Already on her. Marking. Following. When they come—and they will—we'll know."

— THIS IS DANGEROUS

— "Everything is dangerous for people like us."

— SHE WILL SUFFER

— "I know."

— PEOPLE WILL DIE

I close my eyes. "I already carry so many. What would be a few more..."

The lie dies in my throat.

Because it will make a difference. Always does.

But sometimes... sometimes you need to let someone suffer to understand the pattern.

### NARRATOR: NEFELI

It's been four years since Minus destroyed those shadows in the Shadow World. Since he broke the balance.

And since then... nothing.

Neither shadows nor Illuminated Ones approach him. As if his presence created a dead zone around him.

In the first two marked ones we found, Minus stayed close. Watching.

Nothing happened. The special nights never came.

This time will be different.

Minus will stay far away. Will let Red Eyes follow the girl.

And will only appear when the creature gives the signal.

If it does.

### NARRATOR: MINUS

— "We'll keep our distance," I finally say. "Observe. See how they act with her. And when we discover the pattern..."

— WE CHANGE THE RULES

— "We change the rules."

The wind blows. Cold. Dry.

And down there, a brown-skinned girl walks.

Not knowing she's being watched.

Not knowing that tonight might change everything.

Not knowing that in the shadows—always in the shadows—something waits.

### THE GIRL · NARRATOR: LUNA

My name is Luna.

I'm sixteen years old. I live—if I can call it living—in a municipal shelter in North Wing.

Stealing wallets wasn't the plan when I woke up this morning. Never is.

But hunger has a way of changing your principles.

I walk down W3 back to the shelter. The sun is setting. Sky tinted orange and purple.

Beautiful. Not that I stop to appreciate it.

My mind is still on that guy. The one with blue dreads. The one who ignored me. The one who disappeared.

Literally disappeared.

I blinked and he was no longer there.

— "Fatigue," I whisper to myself. "Just fatigue."

But it wasn't.

Because in that second—when the can passed by his head—I saw something.

Blue light. Around his neck. Protective.

Just like...

My hand goes instinctively to my right shoulder. Where, under the jacket, the mark burns.

Always burns.

Since I was born, they say. My biological mother abandoned me at the hospital. "Baby marked by the devil" was scrawled on the note she left.

I grew up in the system. Orphanages. Shelters. Adoptive families who returned me when strange things started happening.

Because they always happen.

The Forces of Darkness. The Bright Ones. The nights when everything goes wrong.

I learned not to talk about it. Learned to pretend I'm normal.

But I'm not.

And that guy with blue dreads... he wasn't either.

I turn onto a smaller street. Shortcut to the shelter. Faster but less lit.

The sun has already set completely. Sky darkening from purple to navy blue to black.

It's been a while since anything happened. Three months since the last strange night. I thought—naively, perhaps—that it was over. That I was finally free.

But then I feel it.

The tingling.

Cold fingers on the nape of my neck. Air getting dense.

— "No," I whisper. "Not today. Please."

But the universe never listens.

I quicken my pace.

The tingling increases. Like a thousand insects crawling under my skin.

The shield—that invisible protection that always surrounded me—begins to pulse.

Recognizing threat.

Run.

### NEWS · NARRATOR: MINUS

The phone glows in my hand. Nefeli watches over my shoulder.

I scroll through the news feed. Brasília. Last 48 hours.

"UNEXPLAINED DEATH IN SOUTH WING - BODY FOUND WITH IMPOSSIBLE INJURIES"

"ENTIRE FAMILY DIES IN SOURCELESS FIRE - INVESTIGATORS PERPLEXED"

"THIRD CASE OF FATAL SELF-MUTILATION IN TWO WEEKS"

I know this pattern. Know it intimately.

Claw marks. Fire that rises instead of falls. Hysterical laughter. Smiling self-mutilation.

Rodrigues family. São Miguel Orphanage.

All over again.

"Last news was six hours ago," I murmur. "Six hours since the last confirmed death."

Nefeli's screen flashes:

— THE INTERVAL IS DECREASING

"Always decreases before a special night," I agree. "As if they're preparing."

I look down. Three blocks south. Where Luna walks.

Where Red Eyes follows her.

— "Tonight," I whisper. "It's going to be tonight."

— ARE YOU SURE?

— "Yes. I'm sure."

I put away the phone. The chain pulses around my arm. Blue and black. Ready.

— "We'll stay distant. We'll observe. We'll learn."

— AND IF SHE DIES?

The question hangs in the air. Heavy. Inevitable.

— "Then we'll learn from her death."

The words come out colder than I intended. But they're true.

And Nefeli—who knows me better than anyone—doesn't question.

Just observes. As she always has.

### THE SIGNAL · NARRATOR: NEFELI

And then I feel it.

Not through Minus. Through Red Eyes.

The connection the creature has with me. Weak. But present.

When Minus gave me a name, something changed between the three of us. Not just Minus and me. Not just Minus and Red Eyes.

The three of us. Connected.

And now I feel the pulse. The urgency. The warning.

I project the screen immediately. Large. Bright:

— THEY ARE APPROACHING

Minus raises his head. Instant.

— "Now?"

— NOW

The chain explodes around him. No longer contained.

Manifest.

Electric blue and deep black serpentining through arm, chest, shoulders. Alive. Pulsing. Ready for war.

— "How much time do we have?"

— MINUTES. MAYBE LESS

Minus looks in the direction. Sees nothing. Too far. But feels.

— "We stay distant," he says. His voice trembles slightly. "We observe. We learn."

I don't respond.

Because we both know.

We won't just observe. We never could.

The chain whips. Grabs onto the opposite rooftop. Pulls Minus forward.

And we begin to move.

Through the city. Over buildings. Between shadows.

Hunter going to hunt.

### PURE TERROR · NARRATOR: LUNA

I run.

Feet pounding on asphalt. Then on dirt. Then on grass.

Lungs burning. Heart exploding in my chest.

I don't stop. Can't stop.

The tingling is worse. Much worse. Like a thousand needles piercing my skin from inside.

The shield pulses. Weak. Broken.

And behind me—always behind—I feel them coming.

The Forces of Darkness crawling. Multiplying. Dozens. Hundreds.

I look up.

And freeze.

Two.

There are two Bright Ones.

I've never seen two in my life. Never. It was always one. Always just one floating alone in the sky ordering the carnage.

But today... today there are two.

Two points of golden light pulsing in the dark sky. Close. Almost touching each other.

And if one Bright One meant massacre—eight dead, twelve dead, twenty dead...

What would two mean?

— "No," I whisper. Voice broken. "No, no, no..."

But they don't hear. Never hear.

The first Force of Darkness emerges from the ground three meters in front of me.

Not crawling. Exploding.

Humanoid form. Tall. Translucent claws. Red eyes fixed on me.

I turn. Run in the other direction.

Another Force.

I turn again. Two more.

They're surrounding me.

And my shield—my damn broken shield—pulses too weak to protect.

Open holes. Enormous cracks.

For the first time in sixteen years... I'm completely vulnerable.

And they know.

I see it in the red eyes. In the way they move. In the hunger in the translucent forms.

Finally they can touch me.

Finally they can destroy me.

Terror explodes. Not fear. Not anxiety.

Pure terror. Primitive. Paralyzing.

But I don't freeze. I run.

Because it's all I know how to do. Always has been.

Through the Forces. Between the claws. Dodging by pure instinct.

A claw tears my jacket. I feel the cold where it almost touched skin.

Another passes so close to my face I feel the air displacing.

And above—always above—the two Bright Ones watch.

Orchestrating. Ordering.

Ensuring that this time... this time I don't escape.

I stumble. Fall. Roll.

Get up running.

Tears flow. Not from sadness. From pure animal terror.

And as I run—as the Forces multiply and the Bright Ones pulse and my shield falls apart piece by piece...

A single question crosses my mind:

Why always me?

Why do I always survive alone?

And why, this time, do I feel I won't?

The Forces of Darkness advance.

The Bright Ones order.

And I run.

Always running.

Toward an end I know—I always knew—would eventually come.

Tonight. With two Bright Ones in the sky.

Tonight is when it all ends.
